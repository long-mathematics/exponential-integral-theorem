## 1. Actual density: branch, orientation, and logarithmic variation

For the fixed phase `q=y^2-x^3+3x` and triangle
`-1/2 <= x <= 1/2`, `|y| <= 1/2-x`, write

\[
f(x)=-x^3+3x,\qquad p(x)=-x^3+x^2+2x+1/4.
\]

Let `r_2` be the middle inverse branch of `f`, let `c(t)` be the increasing
inverse branch of `p` starting in `(-1/5,0)`, and put `a(t)=1/2-c(t)`.
The actual density near zero is

\[
\rho(t)=\int_{-a(t)}^{a(t)}
\frac{dy}{3(1-r_2(t-y^2)^2)}.
\]

The following checks are now part of the proof rather than implicit:

* `p' > 0` on `[-1/5,1]`,
  `p(-1/5)=-51/500`, `p(0)=1/4`,
  `p(4/5)=989/500 < 2 < p(1)=9/4`.
  Thus the endpoint branch is analytic through `0 <= t <= 2`, and
  `c(2) in (4/5,1)`.
* On the continued integration segment,
  `t-y^2 in [f(c(t)),t] subset (-2,2)` for `0<t<2`.
  The inverse branch remains the stated middle branch.
* At `t=11/8`, `a(t)=0`. Substitution `y=a(t)v` writes the integral as
  `a(t)` times a holomorphic integral on `[-1,1]`; the endpoint crossing
  is therefore analytic, not a missing continuation step.
* For `t>11/8`, the continued integral has reversed orientation. Near
  `t=2`, the endpoints are distinct and unramified, with `a(2)<-3/10`.
  The identity `f(1-v)=2-3v^2+v^3` gives
  
  \[
  \rho(2-\epsilon)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt3}\log\epsilon+O(1).
  \]
  In particular the nodal variation is nonzero, with its sign checked on
  the actual oriented integral.

The local annulus description gives
`(T_+-I)rho=c_* h_+`, `c_* != 0`, where `h_+` is the closed vanishing-cycle
period of `dx/(2y)` and is fixed by `T_+`. The relation
`dx/(2y)=-dy/[3(1-x^2)]` fixes the possible sign between the chosen
Gelfand–Leray form and the actual density.

A rational linear boundary expression would imply that a nonzero
constant-coefficient differential operator sends `rho` to an algebraic
phase germ. A power of the local monodromy fixes that algebraic germ,
forcing the same operator to annihilate `h_+`. Such a solution must extend
to an entire function. But at the other node `t=-2`, the standard period
integral contains a segment comparable to

\[
\int_0^{u_0}\frac{du}{\sqrt{u(u+\delta)}}
=2\operatorname{arsinh}\sqrt{u_0/\delta}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

This contradiction proves nonmembership in the **rational linear span**
of the actual face vector. It does not assume nonmembership in its fraction
field. The logarithm at `2` belongs to an analytic continuation of a regular
density germ, not to the original density outside its compact support.

## 2. Picard–Vessiot comparison: replacing the compressed argument

The standalone note used a locally finite orbit argument on a
Picard–Vessiot torsor. The rewritten proof instead uses the following
direct construction.

If `y` is a differentially finite element of a boundary Picard–Vessiot
field, its Galois conjugates span a finite-dimensional constant space `V`.
Choose a basis `y_1,...,y_r`. The Wronskian quotient

\[
\mathcal L_V(g)=
\frac{\operatorname{Wr}(y_1,\ldots,y_r,g)}
     {\operatorname{Wr}(y_1,\ldots,y_r)}
\]

has coefficients fixed by the full boundary group, hence in the base
field. It has a fundamental set inside the boundary field. Differential
spans generated by the original elements are quotients of its coefficient
module. This proves the needed tensor-category membership directly.

For the actual inhomogeneous column, `E/N` is a quotient of the irreducible
coefficient module. Nonzero quotient makes it the whole module. Were all
column entries in the boundary Picard–Vessiot field, its adjoint module
would belong to the boundary tensor category. This explains, without
assuming the desired conclusion, the upgrade from the linear test to the
field-level obstruction.

The normal-subgroup argument now explicitly handles finite components.
For a normal subgroup `H` of a group in `GL_n` containing `SL_n`, either
`H` contains `SL_n`, or `H intersect SL_n` is finite central. In the latter
case the commutator map from the connected group `SL_n` to this finite
intersection is constant, so `H` centralizes `SL_n` and is scalar. Thus a
failure of derived-group survival would trivialize the adjoint over the
boundary field, contradicting projective exclusion.

Finally, an algebraic subgroup of `GL_n` containing `SL_n` is reductive.
The affine translation kernel is either zero or the full standard module.
In the zero case the cocycle splits, and the actual nonzero quotient forces
a nonzero homogeneous vector. Its `SL_n` orbit is dense in affine space.
This is the complete independence argument, not just an invariant-count
heuristic.

## 3. Boundary projective exclusion and finite components

The elliptic homogeneous system has trace `-2/z`, determinant solution
`z^{-2}`, and scalar equation

\[
y''+\frac2z y'+\left(\frac5{36z^2}-4\right)y=0.
\]

Its Bessel order is `1/3`, so its group is `SL_2`. Its adjoint exponential
factors at infinity are `1,e^{4z},e^{-4z}`.

The two slanted faces supply one cubic block. The exact identity

\[
p(\sqrt7\,s/3+1/3)=107/108+
(7\sqrt7/27)(-s^3+3s)
\]

gives its adjoint factors `1,e^{4 beta z},e^{-4 beta z}`, with
`beta=7 sqrt(7)/27 != +/-1`. All other diagonal boundary factors are rank
one. The off-diagonal kernel is unipotent; the reductive quotient has just
one nonabelian simple derived factor, detected by this cubic block.

If the elliptic adjoint belonged to the boundary category, its `PGL_2`
quotient would have to be the cubic projective quotient. After aligning
the restrictions to the simple derived factor, the two maps agree on
every component: the discrepancy at any group element centralizes
`PGL_2`, whose centralizer in itself is trivial. Their adjoint modules
would consequently be isomorphic over `C(z)`, contradicting their formal
exponential factors.

This argument concerns **projective quotients**. It does not claim that
all exponential factors in the entire boundary tensor category lie in one
critical-value difference lattice. Rank-one twists cancel in the adjoint.

## 4. Actual polynomial systems and arithmetic specialization

The spatial polynomial reduction and actual face forcing survive the
audit unchanged in content. The general finiteness input is holonomic
algebraic direct image, not a Milnor-number formula without tameness.
The elliptic rank is established separately by tensoring the two
one-variable polynomial complexes.

The rational Gelfand–Leray certificates and the Weyl substitution identify
the displayed differential presentation. The revised text no longer lets
this be read as a uniqueness assertion about an unspecified holonomic
extension across the phase nodes. The proof uses the actual polynomial
certificates, explicit matrix, and actual density.

The five-coordinate actual face vector is explicitly closed. After adding
the two elliptic coordinates and, where needed, the constant function, the
rational first-order system has no finite pole except `0`. Applying
Siegel–Shidlovskii transcendence-degree equality separately to the boundary
system and the augmented system gives the increase by two at **every**
nonzero algebraic parameter. There is no unspecified exceptional finite
set in the elliptic value theorem.

The direct Beukers lemma proves the local boundary evaluation kernel
`(z-xi)` without a global normal-form or normality assumption. Its
localization is a DVR and its residue field is the actual boundary-value
field. Numerical injectivity is stated on the fiber algebra. In the
elliptic application this is a polynomial algebra in the two surviving
coordinates over the boundary-value field.

## 5. Substantive changes to Paper V

The canonical source filename is preserved. The title is deliberately
changed to:

**Relative Exponential Periods on Polynomial Flags: Polynomial Boundary
Systems and an Elliptic Comparison Theorem.**

The manuscript date is deliberately updated from August 2026 to
**September 2026**. Both authors, their order, the Le Blanc identifier,
and `galizur@gmail.com` are preserved. This is a mathematical revision,
not an editorial claim that all earlier theorem statements are unchanged.

The manuscript now has three parts:

1. Geometric functional comparison: polynomial chains and densities,
   E-functions, antiderivative linearity, face lattices, rooted variation,
   single-phase polynomial relative exactness, Hodge distinctions, and a
   disjoint-block result on a specified common cover.
2. Actual polynomial boundary systems, the actual one-column criterion,
   and the complete explicit elliptic function/value theorem.
3. A direct local boundary lemma, abstract flat specialization, the
   multicolumn affine orbit calculation, and its separately stated
   geometric realization requirements.

The earlier general endpoint-completeness claim is superseded. In
particular:

* Numerical comparison uses the specialized fiber, not the unspecialized
  functional algebra.
* The global boundary-normal-form inference is removed from the argument.
* Arithmetic boundary forcing comes from literal polynomial Stokes, not
  from algebraic support of independently normalized punctual terms.
* General affine-linear completeness, local lattice compatibility, and
  determinant-line realization are separate hypotheses. They are not
  declared equivalent to original relative cocycle compatibility.
* Generic Jacobian correspondences are not silently promoted to
  denominator-free isotypic polynomial comparisons.
* The rational/complex Hodge realization distinction is corrected.
* The disjoint-block hypotheses are imposed on the actual common phase
  cover; irreducibility and nonisomorphism are not assumed to persist
  under an arbitrary finite cover. The rooted rank-one formula allows
  nonzero rational coefficients, including powers of local monodromy.
* The real density lemma explicitly requires a nonconstant phase. The
  algebraic boundary construction does not require a real phase.
* The determinant map is proved free by monic division. Generic orbit
  ideal pullback is justified by flatness and determinant localization.
* Cleared local determinant coefficients are normalized using only units.

The actual elliptic independence theorems do not use Paper III. Paper III
is used only to interpret the residual relations among boundary symbols
as compact polynomial-line formal period operations. General multiphase,
isotypic, higher-rank, and multicolumn geometric applications still require
their own actual comparisons.
