## Disposition of the proposed changes

### 1. Observable relative monodromy and the fixed phase base

The abstract rooted-orbit equality is correct under its stated hypothesis that the group is generated by the displayed rank-one Picard–Lefschetz transformations, and is retained. The geometric application needs less: the full relative variation span **contains** the absolute weight-one homology. Additional face monodromies may enlarge that span. The proof now gives the propagation argument and uses only the required containment.

Condition (A3) is formulated on the chosen smooth phase base, with the finite boundary exceptional set removed and the reduced face divisor finite étale there. It does not require the divisor to split into sections. The text explicitly requires that the relative Picard–Lefschetz action used in the observable datum have the displayed form on this base. It does not infer that action, or preservation of observability and irreducibility, from an unspecified finite cover. This clarifies an actual hypothesis used in the general observable theorem; it does not assert a new automatic observability theorem. The explicit elliptic proof does not depend on that general observable theorem.

### 2. Non-injective parametrizations in the density lemma

Coarea is applied on each original parameter square to `q composed with Phi` and the signed pullback density, including the Jacobian; the pushforwards are then summed with orientation and multiplicity. Thus injectivity of the polynomial map is not used. If the pulled-back two-form is nonzero, the phase on the square is nonconstant and its critical locus has measure zero. The exceptional phase set may be refined for the algebraic relative Gauss–Manin system, and the real face points are placed in their algebraic face divisor for continuation.

### 3. Formal interpretation: a necessary distinction from the preliminary proposal

The proposed promotion of the formal-period remark required checking Paper III itself, not merely changing an environment name. Its main formal theorem is numerical. It must not be cited as an automatic generic formal theorem over a parameter-dependent boundary field.

The new proposition `prop:localized-formal-comparison` consequently separates:

- a functional Stokes presentation over the **actual** boundary field, or the actual local boundary DVR;
- injectivity on the specialized polynomial fiber;
- a numerical formal-period interpretation using the subalgebra inherited from Paper III's formal compact-line category **after specialization**, localized only at elements with nonzero value.

The proof defines the symbol algebra, reduces every amplitude using the existing Laurent-polynomial Stokes certificates, and uses the already proved independence of the two moments to rule out any additional relation. Paper III identifies only the specified specialized coefficient algebra. Boundary relations may use auxiliary objects allowed in Paper III's category; a smaller faces-only proof syntax is not asserted.

This is a localized fixed-chain comparison, not an unlocalized cancellation theorem, not a new generic formal boundary theorem, and not a theorem for additional chains or arbitrary primitive blocks. The introduction and README state this boundary explicitly. None of the actual independence proofs acquires a dependency on Paper III or Paper IV.

### 4. Affine cocycles and the appendix orbit theorem

A short regular-cocycle lemma makes the splitting step explicit. The action on `V plus the trivial representation` and complete reducibility give the coboundary. Finite component groups are included by averaging, rather than silently assuming connectedness. The sign convention is checked against `sigma(Y)=Y g_sigma`: writing `c_sigma=g_sigma C-C` makes `U-YC` fixed.

The appendix keeps its theorem status and supplies the missing details: the independent product of special-linear factors; decomposition of the translation kernel in column multiplicity spaces; affine-linear elimination and reductive splitting; rectangular full-rank versus square determinant orbits; the full character lattice, including disconnected determinant images; contraction from Laurent coordinates; flatness under determinant pullback; reducedness and density of the open orbit; and faithfully flat descent from the Picard–Vessiot torsor. It distinguishes the base-changed torsor coordinate map from evaluation at one point of a splitting field. The existing elementary free-determinant lemma supplies the required flatness. No generic invariant is thereby assigned an unproved geometric/formal realization.

The number of square blocks is denoted `m` separately from the column rank `s` in the orbit calculation.

### 5. Hodge and Jacobian corrections

The full-faithfulness citation is corrected to Deligne, **Théorie de Hodge II**, Rappel (4.4.3), not Hodge III. To avoid an unjustified global choice of sections after base change, the divisor is constructed over a finite extension of the generic function field, traced down with rational coefficients, and closed in the smooth total product. Its action agrees with the specified local-system map on a dense open and hence everywhere.

The uniqueness statement now explicitly has a connected smooth complex algebraic (therefore compactifiable) base. The complex-intertwiner corollary separates the rational-VHS statement from its Jacobian-correspondence conclusion: the latter applies in the curve-family setting of the preceding proposition. These are scope clarifications in the retained Hodge section, not changes to the elliptic theorem.

The formal-factor exclusion also has a direct check in the revised proof: a Laurent-series rank-one intertwiner cannot have a nonzero constant term in its logarithmic derivative at infinity. The unequal exponential slopes of the elliptic and cubic adjoints therefore cannot be changed by a rational gauge.

The general polynomial boundary theorem still uses the rank of generic twisted de Rham cohomology, not a Milnor-number formula without hypotheses. The modified-Bessel substitution is a nonzero constant rescaling of the ordinary Bessel variable; the algebraic rank-one twist does not change the connected derived group.
