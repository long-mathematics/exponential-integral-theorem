## Executive verdict

I found no fatal contradiction in the central all-degree argument. The core theorem has a coherent proof chain:

1. import the polynomial moment systems and their semisimplicity/residue data from Paper I;
2. adjoin the finitely many exponential functions forced by endpoints and constant shifts;
3. prove that horizontal endomorphisms over the resulting exponential field are Laurent polynomials in those exponentials;
4. analyze the affine Picard--Vessiot group of the inhomogeneous moment system;
5. split the moment coordinates into an affine-linear constrained block and a full translation block;
6. conclude that the entire functional polynomial-relation ideal is generated by affine-linear relations;
7. descend this assertion from complex to algebraic coefficients;
8. use Beukers' lifting theorem at `z=1` and the linear formal-period theorem of Paper II.

The strongest remaining verification risk is the affine Picard--Vessiot splitting and its descent over the Laurent exponential ring. I re-derived each interface and found it coherent, but this is a genuinely load-bearing differential-Galois argument and should receive an independent specialist audit before submission. The proof also inherits Paper I's Fourier--Laplace semisimplicity input and Paper II's formal linear classification.

The original manuscript was not one paper in an efficient sense. It combined four largely independent developments:

- the all-degree polynomial Kontsevich--Zagier theorem;
- the explicit degree-at-most-two period algebra;
- tensor-square residue resonance through quartics;
- algebraic independence of `Ein`-values and of `pi` with `Ein(1+i)`.

Only the first is required for Paper III's main theorem. The revised main paper is therefore devoted to it. The other results are preserved in a separate, compilable supplementary source.

## Principal corrections and improvements

### 1. The paper now has one theorem-level purpose

The original title and abstract advertised polynomial relations, quadratic normal forms, tensor resonance, `Ein`, and a mixed ordinary/exponential-period theorem simultaneously. This obscured the central result and made the dependency chain look much longer than it is.

The new title is:

> **A Polynomial Kontsevich--Zagier Theorem for Compact Exponential Periods on the Affine Line**

The main paper proves one result: injectivity of the period map on the commutative formal algebra generated by the affine-line category of Paper II. Quadratic, quartic, and `Ein` results are no longer interleaved with its proof.

### 2. Papers I--III now use the same vocabulary

The main and supplementary sources use:

- polynomial `q` or regular function;
- polynomial differential `r(x) dx`;
- finite degree-zero zero-cycle;
- compact relative one-chain;
- exponential period function;
- affine-line formal period space;
- constant-shift identity;
- moment connection and affine Picard--Vessiot group.

The revised sources contain no occurrences of the provisional terms

- `phase`;
- `amplitude`;
- `polynomial-line`;
- `zero-chain`;
- `multiphase`.

`Resonance` is retained only in the supplementary tensor-square discussion, where integral differences of residue eigenvalues are the standard mathematical phenomenon being described.

### 3. Extraneous algebraic evaluation parameters were removed

The main theorem and its value-transfer theorem are stated at `z=1`. A common nonzero algebraic parameter `xi` is absorbed by

\[
q\longmapsto \xi q,
\qquad
\lambda\longmapsto \xi\lambda.
\]

No strength is lost. The parameter `z` remains because it is the deformation variable required for the moment system, functional relations, and Beukers lifting.

The same simplification was applied to the retained results:

\[
H_\beta(\xi)=H_{\xi\beta}(1),
\]

and a value `Ein(alpha xi)` is simply an `Ein`-value at another nonzero algebraic argument. The headline theorems are therefore stated directly for the values at algebraic arguments, rather than with two redundant parameters.

### 4. Unnecessary finitely generated groups were removed from theorem statements

The quadratic and `Ein` results are now stated over

\[
\mathbb E=\overline{\mathbb Q}
   (e^\lambda:\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb Q}).
\]

Every algebraic relation uses only finitely many coefficients, so the proof still reduces internally to a finitely generated additive subgroup. This keeps the theorem statements intrinsic while retaining the finite torus argument in the proof.

### 5. The moment-system convention is aligned with Paper I

The original Paper III imported the older normalized formula with a factor `1/(nz)` and repeatedly discussed affine normalization of `q`. The revised paper uses the audited Paper I package directly:

\[
\mathbf U'
 =\left(C+\frac Dz\right)\mathbf U
  +\frac1z\sum_\lambda \mathbf b_\lambda e^{\lambda z}.
\]

Degree-dependent constants are absorbed into the forcing vectors. No translation, source scaling, or leading-coefficient normalization is introduced.

### 6. The formal algebra is defined once

The original source separately defined a degree-at-most-two boundary algebra and an unrestricted boundary algebra, while also relying on Paper II's linear presentation. The revised main paper starts with Paper II's already-audited affine-line formal vector space and takes its symmetric algebra modulo only the genuinely new multiplicative identities:

\[
E(0)=1,
\qquad E(\lambda)E(\mu)=E(\lambda+\mu),
\qquad E(\lambda)P(q,r,\Delta)=P(q+\lambda,r,\Delta).
\]

The degree-two presentation is preserved in the supplementary file because it gives an additional explicit normal form, not because it is needed to define the general algebra.

### 7. An ambiguous comparison claim was removed

The original and intermediate drafts referred to a general "compact exponential Kontsevich--Zagier algebra" without defining that full higher-dimensional algebra in the paper. The revised theorem makes the precise claim that is proved: injectivity on the tensor-generated formal algebra built from Paper II's affine-line formal space. The tensor-separable higher-dimensional corollary is stated concretely through Fubini. No undefined ambient algebra is needed.

### 8. Literature positioning was corrected

The revised introduction now distinguishes the theorem from:

- Beukers' lifting of arbitrary polynomial relations at nonsingular algebraic points;
- Adamczewski--Rivoal and Bostan--Rivoal--Salvy on exceptional values and minimal differential equations;
- Fischler--Rivoal's algorithms computing functional and value relation ideals for a fixed tuple of `E`-functions.

The new claim is not mere decidability or specialization. It is the uniform structural assertion that, for this whole geometric family and after adjoining the required exponential shifts, the functional polynomial ideal is generated by its affine-linear part, followed by Paper II's formal geometric classification of those generators.

The Commelin--Habegger--Huber citation is updated to arXiv:2007.08280v4, revised 28 March 2025 and marked to appear in *Annales de l'Institut Fourier*. Companion-paper titles now match the audited Papers I and II.

## Audit of the central proof

### A. Exponential-field horizontal endomorphisms

Let

\[
K_\Gamma=\mathbb C(z,e^{\omega_1z},\ldots,e^{\omega_rz})
\]

for a basis of a finitely generated additive subgroup. The torus acts by independently rescaling the exponential generators. The solution space of

\[
P'=[C+D/z,P]
\]

inside `M_N(K_Gamma)` is finite-dimensional over the constants and torus-stable. Hence every solution has a finite weight expansion

\[
P=\sum_\mu e^{\mu z}R_\mu(z),
\qquad R_\mu\in M_N(\mathbb C(z)).
\]

Pole comparison excludes finite poles away from zero. At zero, a pole of order `m` would force `-m` into the spectrum of `ad D`; all residue differences lie in `(-1,1)`. Thus `R_mu` is polynomial.

At infinity, the leading block maps a `C`-eigenspace of eigenvalue `alpha` to one of eigenvalue `beta`, with `beta-alpha=mu`. The next coefficient equation is

\[
dR=\overline D_\beta R-R\overline D_\alpha.
\]

Every eigenvalue of the operator on the right is a difference of numbers in `(-1,0)`, hence lies in `(-1,1)` and cannot be the positive integer `d`. Therefore each `R_mu` is constant and

\[
[C,R_\mu]=\mu R_\mu,
\qquad [D,R_\mu]=0.
\]

This proves the required Laurent-polynomial normal form for horizontal projectors. The constant-shift example `U_{q+c}=e^{cz}U_q` confirms that ordinary constant-projector rigidity would be false after exponential base change.

### B. Semisimplicity after exponential base change

Let `L_h/C(z)` be the homogeneous Picard--Vessiot field and intersect it with `K_Gamma` in a common differential overfield. Since `K_Gamma/C(z)` has torus Galois group, every intermediate subgroup is normal and the intersection extension is Picard--Vessiot. The base-changed Galois group identifies with a closed normal subgroup of the original reductive group. In characteristic zero it is reductive, so the homogeneous module remains semisimple.

This argument is standard Picard--Vessiot theory, but the precise intersection/base-change identification is an interface worth having checked by a differential-Galois specialist. The revised proof states the field intersection and the group identification explicitly instead of merely asserting that semisimplicity survives extension of scalars.

### C. Affine Picard--Vessiot exact sequence

For `u'=Au+f`, the augmented Picard--Vessiot group fits into

\[
1\to W\to\widetilde H\to H\to1,
\]

where `W` is an `H`-stable vector subgroup of the homogeneous solution space. Surjectivity of restriction is proved by fixed fields. After choosing an `H`-stable complement, the complementary translation term is a regular algebraic `1`-cocycle. Because a reductive group over `C` is linearly reductive, this cocycle is a coboundary.

No tensor construction is needed here. This is the key conceptual improvement over the tensor-by-tensor route: the affine orbit is analyzed before taking polynomial functions on it.

### D. Laurent-ring splitting and Swan's theorem

An `H`-equivariant projector onto `W` produces the horizontal matrix

\[
P=YEY^{-1}\in M_N(K_\Gamma).
\]

The horizontal-endomorphism theorem places `P` in the Laurent ring `M_N(A_Gamma)`. Its image and kernel are finitely generated projective modules. Swan's theorem makes them free, allowing a gauge

\[
S\in\operatorname{GL}_N(A_\Gamma)
\]

that transforms `P` to a standard diagonal projector.

The revised proof now explains the simultaneous block diagonalization of the homogeneous fundamental matrix. After a constant change of solution basis, `E` is the same standard projector; with `B=SY`, the identity

\[
BEB^{-1}=E
\]

forces `B` to be block diagonal.

Because the determinant of an invertible Laurent-polynomial matrix is a Laurent monomial times a nonzero constant, both `S` and `S^{-1}` are entire and invertible at zero. This is used when applying the local meromorphic-solution exclusion.

### E. Constrained coordinates lie in the ring, not merely its fraction field

The complementary affine cocycle is a coboundary, so the constrained block differs from a base-field vector by a homogeneous solution. That homogeneous solution is single-valued and meromorphic at zero. Since the moment residue spectrum contains no integer, it vanishes. Thus the constrained coordinates first lie in `K_Gamma`.

The horizontal complementary projector `Q` then gives

\[
\mathscr L(Q\mathbf U)=Q\mathbf f.
\]

The right side has finitely many torus weights, while `mathscr L` is injective and torus-equivariant. Therefore `Q U` has a finite weight expansion. Pole comparison shows that every rational coefficient is polynomial, proving

\[
Q\mathbf U\in R_\Gamma^N.
\]

This ring-level conclusion is essential: it prevents a hidden localization from appearing in the functional ideal.

### F. The translation block is algebraically independent

The pure translation group acts on the free block by

\[
\mathbf V_W\mapsto \mathbf V_W+B_Wc.
\]

A polynomial relation over the base field would remain valid under every translation. Since `B_W` is invertible, this gives a polynomial vanishing under an invertible affine change for every `c`, hence the zero polynomial. Thus the free coordinates are algebraically independent and the functional ideal is exactly the ideal of the affine-linear constrained coordinates.

### G. Descent to algebraic coefficients

All Taylor coefficients lie in `k=Qbar`. The map

\[
\mathbb C\otimes_k k[[z]]\to\mathbb C[[z]]
\]

is injective: for a finite tensor sum, choose the complex coefficients linearly independent over `k` and compare every Taylor coefficient. Flatness identifies the complex kernel with scalar extension of the algebraic kernel. Faithful flatness then descends the equality between the full ideal and the ideal generated by its affine-linear part. Noetherianity supplies finitely many generators.

### H. Beukers lifting and formal specialization

A polynomial value relation involves finitely many moments and exponential indices. Adjoin the constant function and the necessary exponential coordinates, homogenize, and apply Beukers at `z=1`. Products of exponential coordinates are then collapsed in the group algebra by addition of indices. The lifted relation belongs to the functional ideal and hence to the ideal generated by affine-linear functional relations.

At `z=1`, a term

\[
e^\mu U_{q,s,\Delta}(1)
\]

is exactly the affine-line period with polynomial `q+mu`. Therefore each specialized affine-linear generator is a linear numerical relation in the category of Paper II and is formal there. Polynomial multiples vanish in the symmetric-algebra quotient. This completes the injectivity proof.

## Audit of the retained results

### Quadratic integral values

The identity

\[
2zH_\beta'(z)+H_\beta(z)=e^{\beta z}
\]

and the substitution

\[
Y_\beta(w)=wH_\beta(w^2)
          =\int_0^w e^{\beta u^2}\,du
\]

reduce algebraic dependence to the Kolchin--Ostrowski antiderivative criterion. Torus-character extraction isolates one equation

\[
r'+2\beta wr=c,
\]

which has no rational solution. The functional and value-independence arguments are coherent, and the algebraic evaluation parameter was removed from the theorem statement.

The explicit degree-two formal normal form follows from twisted reduction, completing the square, and the substitution from a general endpoint to `[0,1]`. It yields

\[
\overline{\mathbb Q}[(\overline{\mathbb Q},+)]
 [T_\beta:\beta\ne0].
\]

This result is strong and quotable, but it is not needed for the all-degree theorem because the latter uses affine Picard--Vessiot linearity rather than explicit generators.

### Tensor-square residue analysis

For polynomial degrees two and three, tensor residue eigenvalues lie in an interval of width `2/3`, excluding integral commutator eigenvalues both at zero and at infinity. The constancy proof is valid.

For quartics, the matrices `C`, `D`, the induced symmetric-square matrix, the pole equation, and the contradiction in the non-pure case were independently checked symbolically. In the pure case the constant part is scalar; grouping tensor residue weights modulo integers gives the stated matrix-algebra decomposition. The supplement now states the row-module convention explicitly so that the factors `z` and `z^{-1}` have unambiguous orientation.

This section is a structural refinement, not part of the proof of the polynomial relation theorem. Its main lesson is that tensor-square endomorphism resonance does not imply a polynomial equation on the inhomogeneous affine orbit.

### Entire exponential integrals and the mixed theorem

For `E_alpha(z)=Ein(alpha z)`, the equation

\[
zE_\alpha'(z)=1-e^{-\alpha z}
\]

and Kolchin--Ostrowski reduce a proposed dependence to

\[
r'-\alpha r=c/z,
\]

which has no rational solution. Beukers specialization gives algebraic independence of distinct nonzero algebraic `Ein`-values over the field generated by all algebraic exponential values.

The field-intersection lemma and complex conjugation then show that every real transcendental number is algebraically independent from `Ein(alpha)` when `alpha` is nonreal algebraic. Taking `alpha=1+i` and the Lindemann transcendence of `pi` yields the stated mixed result. This proof is independent of the all-degree affine-line theorem and is best treated as a standalone note or a separately cited supplement.

## Assessment of Claude's comments

### Coherent research program

Agree. Papers I--III are successive uses of one mechanism:

- Paper I establishes arithmetic rigidity and the moment connection;
- Paper II classifies linear relations formally;
- Paper III proves that the full polynomial functional ideal is generated by its linear part and therefore upgrades Paper II to all polynomial relations.

The revised Paper III makes this progression more visible by removing unrelated results from the main chain.

### Bridge between three literatures

Agree. The work genuinely combines the `E`-function specialization theory, formal/exponential periods, and polynomial-moment/Cauchy-transform geometry. In Paper III the new bridge is primarily between `E`-function specialization, affine differential Galois theory, and Paper II's formal-period classification.

### Risk of insufficient contact with the existing literature

Agree. The omission of Fischler--Rivoal's effective algebraic-independence theorem was especially significant for Paper III. Their result already computes functional and value relation ideals for a fixed tuple of `E`-functions. Paper III must therefore emphasize the distinct contribution: a uniform geometric family, a proof that its functional ideal is generated by affine-linear relations after exponential shifts, and a formal-period interpretation of the specialized generators.

Direct expert feedback from Beukers, Fischler, Rivoal, or Delaygue remains a high-leverage verification and positioning step.

### Escalation and falling marginal value per page

Agree. The uploaded source was 36 pages and presented four independent theorem packages as one paper. That made the verification burden appear cumulative even though the all-degree theorem does not use the quadratic, tensor-square, or `Ein` sections. The split into a 12-page main theorem and a 9-page retained-results note directly addresses this problem.

### Terminology inflation

Agree. The revised Papers I--III now avoid provisional vocabulary and use standard terms from the adjacent literatures. The central claims can be stated without bespoke shorthand.

### Fischler--Rivoal is close to the core

Agree, with an important distinction. Fischler--Rivoal give an effective algorithm for a fixed tuple. Paper III claims a uniform structural theorem for an infinite geometric category and a Kontsevich--Zagier formal classification. The paper should not suggest that arbitrary-degree lifting or relation-ideal computability is new; the linear-generation theorem and formal interpretation are the claimed additions.

### The degree-two ring and `pi`/`Ein` results are highly visible assets

Agree. They are perhaps more immediately quotable than the affine Picard--Vessiot theorem. That is a reason to preserve and eventually publish them separately, not a reason to bury the main all-degree proof beneath them.

### Journal and field-impact predictions

These are strategic opinions rather than mathematical audit findings. The current source-level audit supports describing the results precisely, but it cannot certify priority, reception, or journal placement. Specialist feedback is required for that calibration.

## Remaining risks and recommended independent checks

1. **Paper I semisimplicity.** Paper III inherits the Fourier--Laplace semisimplicity theorem. That remains the least elementary input in the series.
2. **Picard--Vessiot base change.** A specialist should verify the intersection `L_h cap K_Gamma`, normality, and base-changed Galois-group identification in the exact disconnected-group generality used.
3. **Affine extension and cocycle regularity.** The proof is coherent, but an expert should check the algebraic-group regularity of the descended cocycle and the chosen conventions for affine fundamental matrices.
4. **Swan descent and the gauge.** Verify that the precise form of Swan's Laurent-polynomial freeness theorem applies to both projector summands and that the chosen bases give the asserted global `GL_N` gauge.
5. **Priority.** A literature search found no identical uniform theorem, but search cannot establish novelty. Direct contact with specialists is appropriate before public priority claims.
6. **Companion cross-references.** Final theorem and section numbers should be inserted only after Papers I and II are frozen.
