# Paper IV strengthening audit

## Manuscript

**Title:** *Relative Exponential Periods on Polynomial Flags: Face Lattices, Stokes Generation, and Endpoint-Complete Relations*  
**Author:** Christopher D. Long  
**Date:** August 2026

## Executive conclusion

The manuscript has been strengthened from a constant-coefficient functional comparison theorem with a conditional arithmetic endpoint into a complete value-level polynomial comparison theorem on an explicitly defined **endpoint-complete Stokes--Lefschetz locus**.

The original logical limitation was that Beukers lifting produces polynomial coefficients in the exponential parameter, while the outer-variable Kolchin--Ostrowski theorem does not control relations created after endpoint evaluation. The revised paper no longer tries to bridge these two statements directly. It retains the phase pushforward as a compactly supported distribution, completes its relative Gauss--Manin system by the face and punctual constituents, computes the primitive Fourier--Laplace Stokes group, determines the resulting affine differential-Galois orbit ideal, and proves local flat descent at the chosen algebraic parameter.

The resulting numerical theorem is unconditional **under the endpoint-complete hypotheses (E1)--(E5)**. The paper does not assert that every admissible observable polynomial flag automatically satisfies those stronger hypotheses.

## Principal new results

### 1. Endpoint-complete Stokes--Lefschetz locus

The new definition separates the precise geometric and differential-Galois requirements:

- admissible observable relative geometry;
- a primitive middle-extension module with a basis of finite vanishing cycles and no extra weight-one infinity factor;
- a strongly connected rank-one Stokes root quiver whose roots are separated by the exponential torus;
- boundary-field disjointness up to the central determinant quotient and algebraic Jacobian correspondences;
- formal control of the relative extension or normal-function classes.

This is stronger than the earlier primitive observable Lefschetz locus. It is designed exactly to make endpoint specialization theorem-level rather than conjectural.

### 2. Completed phase distribution

For each flag datum the paper introduces

\[
  \langle\Theta_{D,q,\Omega},\varphi\rangle
  =\int_D\varphi(q)\Omega,
  \qquad
  F_{D,q,\Omega}(z)=\mathcal L(\Theta_{D,q,\Omega})(z).
\]

The Weyl identities

\[
  \mathcal L(tT)=\partial_z\mathcal L(T),
  \qquad
  \mathcal L(\partial_tT)=-z\mathcal L(T)
\]

identify multiplication by powers of the exponential parameter with differentiation on the phase side. Interior terms remain in the middle-extension Gauss--Manin module. Newly point-supported terms are finite sums of derivatives of delta distributions and transform to polynomial multiples of algebraic exponentials, so they lie in the zero-dimensional compact boundary category.

### 3. Actual boundary-function algebra

The coefficient field is not an arbitrary Picard--Vessiot enlargement. It is the fraction field of the algebra generated by the actual compact polynomial-line face functions, punctual exponentials, their required differential coordinates, and the parameter.

Using the complete polynomial-line theorem, the paper obtains the normal form

\[
  \mathcal R_\partial
  \simeq k[z]\otimes_k k[\Gamma][T_1,\ldots,T_m].
\]

Thus the boundary algebra is a normal noetherian domain. At a nonsingular nonzero algebraic point \(\xi\), its evaluation kernel is exactly \((z-\xi)\), and the height-one localization is a discrete valuation ring.

### 4. Stokes generation of the derived group

The D'Agnolo--Hien--Morando--Sabbah Fourier formula identifies the off-diagonal Stokes data with the vanishing-cycle quiver. The revised paper proves:

- torus conjugation extracts the individual root lines from each Stokes logarithm;
- a strongly connected directed root graph generates \(\mathfrak{sl}_n\);
- Ramis density promotes this to containment of \(\mathrm{SL}_n\) in the differential Galois group;
- boundary-field base change preserves the derived group under (E4);
- projectively disjoint blocks yield the product of their special-linear derived groups, with all residual coupling measured by determinant characters.

A separate lemma proves reductivity of the primitive homogeneous groups from simplicity and the normal-unipotent-radical argument.

### 5. Complete affine orbit ideal

For an inhomogeneous system

\[
  U'=AU+B,
\]

the affine Picard--Vessiot group has a translation kernel followed by the homogeneous group. The paper proves a complete reduction:

- translation-active columns form free affine-space factors;
- the remaining cocycle splits because the homogeneous group is reductive;
- split columns have the form \(A_0+YC\);
- if the column rank is below the primitive rank, the \(\mathrm{SL}_n\)-orbit is dense and gives no polynomial relation;
- a full-rank square block leaves only its determinant coordinate.

For several saturated blocks, all nonlinear relations are the partial-character binomials attached to

\[
  \Lambda_{\det}
  =\{a\in\mathbf Z^s:\chi^a|_{T_{\det}}=1\}.
\]

The use of the full joint determinant lattice repairs the possible coupling of central characters across different primitive blocks.

### 6. Formal determinant-line realization

Each determinant binomial is realized by exterior powers of the completed systems and the corresponding horizontal rank-one determinant-line identification. Clearing coefficients in the actual boundary algebra produces a formal tensor relation. This places the exceptional full-rank equations inside the stated formal period operations rather than treating them as unexplained differential-Galois invariants.

### 7. Local flat endpoint descent

The revised paper proves that denominator clearing cannot create a new relation at \(z=\xi\):

- the affine shifts are regular in the boundary DVR;
- the determinant-character coefficients are units;
- partial-character lattice quotients are free over the DVR;
- the determinant morphism is flat by miracle flatness;
- the full affine orbit quotient is flat;
- consequently the generic relation ideal contracts to the local relation ideal.

This is the new terminality bridge between functional classification and endpoint evaluation.

### 8. Numerical endpoint-complete theorem

Beukers lifting now lands in a locally classified functional ideal. After clearing only boundary denominators known to be nonzero at \(\xi\), the lifted relation is generated by the formal completed operations and specializes back to the prescribed numerical relation.

The numerical period map is therefore injective on the local endpoint-complete formal algebra.

### 9. Strong one-layer corollary

For one endpoint-complete flag layer, a nonzero rational weight-one block has even rank at least two, while the cyclic first-order closure supplies only one affine particular-solution column. After removal of any trivial affine-linear split column, one column cannot saturate the primitive rank. Hence no determinant-character relation occurs.

Accordingly, every numerical polynomial relation for one layer is generated by the completed linear operations and the complete compact polynomial-line boundary theorem. This is the direct resolution of the endpoint limitation that motivated the strengthening.

## Bounded falsification checks

The paper retains the disconnected-fibre counterexample and the outer-endpoint counterexample. It adds two checks for the new mechanisms:

1. the \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) orbit of one nonzero column is dense, whereas two independent columns first create a determinant equation;
2. the triangular elliptic phase
   \[
     q(x,y)=y^2-x^3+3x
   \]
   has two finite nodal critical values \(\pm2\), a rank-two affine homology basis, a two-arrow Stokes quiver, and no extra vanishing cycle at infinity. Its cubic boundary block has critical-value difference \(28\sqrt7/27\), whose integral difference lattice does not contain \(4\); this excludes a common projective tensor quotient with the elliptic primitive block. The triangle therefore supplies an explicit endpoint-complete one-layer example.

## Repairs made during the final audit

- The initial symplectic-invariant shortcut was discarded: products become convolutions after Fourier--Laplace, and rooted Picard--Lefschetz spanning alone does not determine the affine translation group.
- The primitive group is generated through the Stokes root quiver and the exponential torus instead.
- Central determinant characters are treated jointly across all blocks; separate determinant equations per block would be incorrect in the presence of central coupling.
- The coefficient field is the fraction field of the actual boundary function algebra, ensuring that every affine shift and coefficient is formally represented.
- Endpoint descent is local at \(\xi\), so no unsafe global denominator cancellation is used.
- A trivial split one-column block is removed by the affine-linear reduction before the dense-orbit argument; the one-column corollary now states this correctly.
- The one-layer conclusion is obtained by restricting the injective full formal algebra to the original flag and boundary subalgebra, not by an unjustified algebraic elimination of derivative coordinates.

## Exact scope

The paper now proves the complete numerical polynomial theorem for endpoint-complete collections. It still does **not** claim:

- that every primitive observable polynomial phase satisfies (E2)--(E5);
- a theorem for several unobserved places at infinity without an additional puncture or 1-motive analysis;
- a rank-one root-quiver theorem for resonant or repeated exponential factors;
- automatic control of unrestricted isomorphic relative normal functions;
- a theorem for genuinely complex-valued phases with two-dimensional phase support.

These are stated as boundaries of the theorem rather than hidden assumptions.
