\begin{abstract}
Let $k=\Qbar$ and $k_{\R}=k\cap\R$.  We study compact two-variable
exponential integrals
\[
  F_{D,q,\Omega}(z)=\int_D e^{zq}\Omega
\]
for polynomially parametrized real flag chains $D\subset\R^2$, real
algebraic polynomial phases $q\in k_{\R}[x,y]$, and polynomial two-forms
$\Omega$.  On the primitive observable Lefschetz locus we prove the
geometric comparison theorem developed in the first part of the paper:
vanishing of a common-phase function forces polynomial relative exactness,
so the two-dimensional integral reduces, without denominators, to compact
polynomial-line periods on the faces.  We also identify the balanced face
lattice with the toric quotient in relative homology, prove exact rooted
Picard--Lefschetz observability, and realize irreducible weight-one
intertwiners by relative Jacobian correspondences.

The second part closes the arithmetic endpoint problem on a stronger,
explicitly testable sublocus, called the endpoint-complete
Stokes--Lefschetz locus.  The phase pushforward is retained as a compactly
supported distribution in a completed relative Gauss--Manin module.
Fourier--Laplace transformation converts multiplication by the exponential
parameter into distributional differentiation.  The interior singular
behavior remains in the middle extension, while every newly supported
endpoint or critical-value term is a punctual constituent and hence a compact
polynomial-line boundary object.  The Stokes multipliers of the primitive
Fourier transform are read from the vanishing-cycle quiver.  A strongly
connected root quiver, together with the exponential torus, generates
$\SL$ of the primitive solution space.  Differential Galois theory then
computes the complete affine orbit ideal of any finite family of flag
solutions.  Translation-active columns are free; split columns have no
relations below full rank; and the only full-rank relations are the toric
binomials of the joint determinant-character lattice.

The coefficient field is the fraction field of the actual compact
polynomial-line boundary-function algebra.  The complete polynomial-line
theorem makes this boundary algebra normal and controls its specialization.
Localizing at a nonsingular nonzero algebraic parameter gives a discrete
valuation ring.  The affine orbit quotient is flat there, so no new
parameter-polynomial relation appears on endpoint evaluation.  Beukers'
refined Siegel--Shidlovskii theorem therefore yields a complete numerical
polynomial Kontsevich--Zagier theorem for endpoint-complete collections.
For one endpoint-complete flag layer the primitive rank is either zero or
at least two, while there is only one affine column; consequently the
full-rank determinant case cannot occur.  Thus every numerical polynomial
relation for one layer is generated solely by algebraic pullback and trace,
completed twisted Stokes, face and punctual relations, Jacobian
correspondences when present, and the complete compact polynomial-line
theorem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and main results}\label{sec:intro}

Kontsevich and Zagier proposed that numerical relations between periods are
generated by additivity, algebraic change of variables, and Stokes' formula
\cite{KontsevichZagier}.  In the exponential setting a differential form
$\omega$ is replaced by $e^f\omega$, and the de Rham differential becomes
$d_f=d+df\wedge$.  Several formal, cohomological, and motivic definitions of
exponential periods are compared in \cite{CommelinHabeggerHuber}.

The one-dimensional compact polynomial-line category is unusually rigid.
The companion paper \cite{LongLinearKZ} proves its linear
Kontsevich--Zagier theorem, and \cite{LongPolynomialKZ} proves the complete
polynomial theorem in that category.  A direct extension to two variables is
not automatic.  Three new phenomena occur:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.4em]
\item a sliced two-chain gives a relative curve cycle with moving endpoints;
\item the affine fibre has a weight-one Jacobian part and boundary or infinity
      lattices;
\item Beukers lifting produces polynomial coefficients in the exponential
      parameter, while relative antiderivative linearity concerns the outer
      integration variable.
\end{enumerate}
The third point is a genuine logical distinction.  It prevents a silent
promotion of a relative functional theorem to a complete numerical
polynomial theorem.

The purpose of this paper is to establish the geometric and differential
algebraic comparison, and then to solve the arithmetic endpoint step on the
explicit endpoint-complete Stokes--Lefschetz locus introduced below.

\subsection{Polynomial flag periods}

Put
\[
  k=\Qbar,
  \qquad
  k_{\R}=k\cap\R.
\]
A polynomial flag chain is the pushforward of the standard square by a
polynomial map
\[
  \Phi:[0,1]^2\longrightarrow\R^2,
  \qquad
  \Phi\in k_{\R}[u,v]^2,
\]
with the induced orientation and multiplicities.  This includes ordinary
triangles, rectangles, and graph flags
\[
  a\le x\le b,
  \qquad
  A(x)\le y\le B(x),
  \qquad A,B\in k_{\R}[x].
\]
For $q,r\in k_{\R}[x,y]$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-period}
  F_{D,q,r}(z)
  =\int_D r(x,y)e^{zq(x,y)}\,dx\,dy.
\end{equation}
The phase is real on the real chain, so $q(D)$ is a compact interval.  This
one-dimensional phase support is essential for the Cauchy-transform
separation used below.

\begin{theorem}[Polynomial-flag $E$-functions]\label{thm:intro-E}
Every function \eqref{eq:intro-period} is an $E$-function over $k$.
\end{theorem}

The proof combines an elementary height estimate for the Taylor coefficients
with holonomic direct image.  The elementary estimate is included in
\cref{sec:E-functions}.

\subsection{Relative affine-Galois linearity}

Let $(K,D)$ be a differential field with algebraically closed constant field
$C$.  If $Y_i'\in K$, the Kolchin--Ostrowski theorem shows that every
algebraic relation among the antiderivatives $Y_i$ is generated by constant
linear combinations already lying in $K$.

\begin{theorem}[Relative affine-Galois linearity]\label{thm:intro-relative-AG}
Let $Y_1,\ldots,Y_m$ lie in a no-new-constants differential extension of $K$
and satisfy $DY_i=f_i\in K$.  After a matrix
$S\in\operatorname{GL}_m(C)$, the transformed coordinates
$Z=S\mathbf Y$ have the form
\[
  Z_1=g_1,\ldots,Z_t=g_t\in K,
\]
while $Z_{t+1},\ldots,Z_m$ are algebraically independent over $K$.
Equivalently,
\[
  \ker\bigl(K[X_1,\ldots,X_m]\to K[Y_1,\ldots,Y_m]\bigr)
\]
is generated by affine-linear polynomials.
\end{theorem}

This theorem is exact for a variable outer endpoint.  It does not control
relations created after evaluating that endpoint.  The distinction is made
explicit in \cref{rem:endpoint-warning}.

\subsection{Face lattices and observability}

Let $\bar f:\bar{\mathcal C}\to S$ be a smooth proper family of connected
curves, and let $B\subset\bar{\mathcal C}$ be a reduced divisor finite
\'etale over $S$.  Write
\[
  \Fcal_B
  =\ker\left(\pi_*\Z_B\xrightarrow{\deg}\Z_S\right).
\]
It is the balanced local system generated by the moving face points.

\begin{theorem}[Face-lattice comparison]\label{thm:intro-face}
There is a natural exact sequence
\[
  0\longrightarrow R_1\bar f_*\Z
  \longrightarrow R_1\bar f_*(\bar{\mathcal C},B;\Z)
  \longrightarrow\Fcal_B\longrightarrow0.
\]
For the generalized Jacobian $J_B=\Pic^0(\bar{\mathcal C}/S,B)$ one has
\[
  1\longrightarrow T_B\longrightarrow J_B\longrightarrow J\longrightarrow0,
  \qquad
  X^*(T_B)\simeq\Fcal_B.
\]
Thus the simplex face module is the lattice quotient in relative homology and,
dually, the character lattice of the generalized-Jacobian torus.  The two
statements have opposite variance and should not be conflated.
\end{theorem}

The projective weight-one part is controlled by Picard--Lefschetz theory.  If
$\gamma$ is a relative slice class and $\nu_i$ are the relative vanishing
cycles, put an arrow $i\to j$ when the dual vanishing cycle of $j$ pairs
nontrivially with $\nu_i$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact rooted orbit theorem]\label{thm:intro-rooted}
Let $R_\gamma$ be the vertices reachable from those $i$ for which the dual
vanishing cycle pairs nontrivially with $\gamma$.  Then
\[
  \Span\{g\gamma-\gamma:g\in G\}
  =\Span\{\nu_i:i\in R_\gamma\}.
\]
In particular, if the absolute vanishing cycles span the weight-one homology,
their intersection graph is connected, and one incidence is nonzero, then the
monodromy variation of $\gamma$ is the complete weight-one homology.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The primitive observable Lefschetz locus}

The denominator-free theorem requires the following hypotheses.

\begin{definition}[Admissible observable flag datum]\label{def:admissible}
An admissible observable flag datum is a triple $(D,q,\Omega)$ with $D$ a
polynomial flag chain, $q\in k_{\R}[x,y]$, and
$\Omega\in\Omega^2_{k[x,y]/k}$, satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(A\arabic*)},leftmargin=3.2em]
\item $q$ has finite critical locus and every fibre $q^{-1}(t)$ is connected;
\item the generic affine fibre has one place at infinity;
\item after resolving the compactified pencil, every finite singular fibre
      has exactly one ordinary node and the finite critical values are
      distinct; over their complement the fibres form a smooth proper curve
      family, and the face intersections become a finite \'etale divisor after
      a finite base change;
\item the resulting Lefschetz vanishing cycles span $H_1$ of the compactified
      generic fibre and have connected intersection graph;
\item the sliced chain is nonzero on at least one regular phase interval,
      and on every interval on which it is nonzero it has nonzero incidence
      with at least one Lefschetz vanishing cycle.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The connected-fibre and finite-critical-locus assumptions put $q$ in the
primitive quasi-fibered class of Bonnet.  The one-place-at-infinity condition
removes an additional puncture lattice: it identifies the first homology of
the affine generic fibre with that of its compactification.

\begin{theorem}[Single-phase denominator-free functional theorem]
\label{thm:intro-single}
Let $(D,q,\Omega)$ be an admissible observable flag datum and put
\[
  F(z)=\int_D e^{zq}\Omega.
\]
If $F\equiv0$, then there is a polynomial $a\in k[x,y]$ such that
\[
  \Omega=da\wedge dq.
\]
Consequently
\[
  e^{zq}\Omega=d\bigl(e^{zq}a\,dq\bigr)
\]
and
\[
  F(z)=\int_{\partial D}e^{zq}a\,dq.
\]
The boundary relation is a compact polynomial-line functional relation and is
therefore generated by the pullback--Stokes operations of
\cite{LongLinearKZ}.
\end{theorem}

The proof is in \cref{sec:relative-exactness}.  Its algebraic step is
Bonnet's theorem that topological relative exactness equals polynomial
relative exactness for primitive quasi-fibered polynomial maps
\cite{BonnetRelativeExactness}.

\subsection{Multiphase comparison}

For a finite family, take a common punctured phase line $S$ and let $V_j$ be
the weight-one local systems.  The clean unconditional statement is the
disjoint-block case.

\begin{theorem}[Multiphase functional comparison: disjoint blocks]
\label{thm:intro-multiphase}
Let $(D_j,q_j,\Omega_j)$ be admissible observable flag data.  Assume that the
complex local systems $V_{j,\C}$ are absolutely irreducible and pairwise
nonisomorphic.  If
\[
  \sum_{j=1}^N c_j
  \int_{D_j}e^{zq_j}\Omega_j\equiv0,
  \qquad c_j\in k,
\]
then the relation is generated by:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.4em]
\item linearity and algebraic pullback or trace;
\item the polynomial identities $\Omega_j=da_j\wedge dq_j$ and
      phase-horizontal Stokes;
\item balanced face relations and compact polynomial-line functional
      relations on the faces, including their zero-dimensional exponential
      boundary terms.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

For isomorphic blocks a relation can be diagonal across several copies.  The
flat intertwiner is nevertheless Hodge-theoretic.

\begin{theorem}[Weight-one Hodge rigidity]\label{thm:intro-Hodge}
Let $\mathbb V$ and $\mathbb W$ be nonzero polarizable rational variations
of effective weight one whose complex local systems are irreducible.  Every nonzero flat
map $\mathbb V_{\C}\to\mathbb W_{\C}$ is an isomorphism of complex variations
of Hodge structure.  After multiplication by a positive integer it is induced
by a homomorphism of the relative Jacobians, and hence by an algebraic
correspondence between the curve families.
\end{theorem}

Hodge rigidity alone identifies the absolute weight-one systems; it does not
automatically identify the relative slice normal functions.  We therefore
state the extension with an explicit compatibility condition in
\cref{sec:multiphase}.  This is one of the repairs made in passing from a
research program to a theorem.

\subsection{Endpoint-complete polynomial comparison}

The functions in this paper are $E$-functions, so Beukers' refinement of the
Siegel--Shidlovskii theorem lifts a polynomial relation at a nonsingular
nonzero algebraic parameter to a polynomial-coefficient functional relation
of the same degree.  The issue is not the arithmetic lifting itself.  The
issue is to classify the resulting $k[z]$-relation after the outer endpoint
has been evaluated.  Variable-endpoint Kolchin--Ostrowski theory does not do
this: evaluation can create relations which were absent in the differential
field of the outer variable.

We close this gap by retaining the phase pushforward before taking its
Laplace transform.  For a flag datum put
\[
  \langle\Theta_{D,q,\Omega},\varphi\rangle
  =\int_D\varphi(q)\Omega.
\]
It is a compactly supported distribution on the real phase line and
\[
  F_{D,q,\Omega}(z)
  =\langle\Theta_{D,q,\Omega},e^{zt}\rangle.
\]
The Weyl-algebra identities
\[
  \mathcal L(t\Theta)=\partial_z\mathcal L(\Theta),
  \qquad
  \mathcal L(\partial_t\Theta)=-z\mathcal L(\Theta)
\]
show exactly what polynomial coefficients in $z$ do: they differentiate the
phase distribution.  Derivatives of a piecewise Gauss--Manin density have an
interior Gauss--Manin part and distributions supported at critical or
endpoint phase values.  The latter are finite sums of $\delta_a^{(m)}$, whose
Laplace transforms are $(-z)^m e^{az}$; they belong to the
zero-dimensional part of the compact polynomial-line boundary category.

The primitive interior module is controlled by the Stokes group of its
Fourier--Laplace transform.  We use the following geometric locus.

\begin{definition}[Endpoint-complete Stokes--Lefschetz collection]
\label{def:endpoint-complete}
A finite collection $\mathscr C$ of polynomial flag data is
\emph{endpoint-complete Stokes--Lefschetz} when the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(E\arabic*)},leftmargin=3.2em]
\item Every datum is admissible observable in the sense of
      \cref{def:admissible}.
\item The primitive middle-extension Gauss--Manin module on the phase line is
      regular at infinity before Fourier transformation, its finite
      vanishing cycles form a basis of the full affine $H_1$, and it has no
      additional weight-one constituent supported at infinity.
\item In a stationary-phase basis, the nonzero rank-one Stokes arrows form a
      strongly connected directed graph, and the exponential torus separates
      their root characters.
\item Let $L_\partial$ be a differential normal closure of the actual
      compact polynomial-line boundary field over $k(z)$.  For each primitive
      Picard--Vessiot field $L_\lambda$, the Galois quotient cut out by
      $L_\lambda\cap L_\partial$ factors through the central torus of the
      primitive group.  Thus base change to the actual boundary field
      $K_\partial(\mathscr C)$ defined below preserves the derived
      special-linear group.  Distinct
      primitive simple blocks have no common projective quotient after this
      base change except those induced by algebraic Jacobian correspondences.
      Standard--dual identifications and rank-one twists are excluded unless
      an explicit algebraic correspondence supplies them.
\item On every isotypic block the relative slice extension classes satisfy
      the correspondence compatibility of
      \cref{def:relative-compatible}; equivalently, all linear relations among
      the completed affine extension classes are generated by the stated
      correspondence, face, and phase-horizontal Stokes operations.
\end{enumerate}
For one flag layer, \textup{(E5)} follows from rooted observability: a
nonzero primitive slice has nonzero variation, while a zero primitive slice
is already reduced to the boundary by polynomial relative Stokes.  The
boundary-disjointness clause of \textup{(E4)} remains to be checked.
\end{definition}

Condition \textup{(E2)} makes the completed Fourier transform a finite
meromorphic connection whose exponential factors are indexed by the finite
critical values.  The topological Stokes formula of
D'Agnolo--Hien--Morando--Sabbah identifies its off-diagonal multipliers with
the vanishing-cycle quiver.  Condition \textup{(E3)} and the exponential
torus then generate every elementary root subgroup, hence the full special
linear group of each primitive block.  Ramis density promotes this Stokes
calculation to the differential Galois group.

Let $\Rcal_\partial(\mathscr C)$ be the finite functional algebra generated
by all compact polynomial-line face functions, all punctual exponential
terms required by the completed module, their differential closure, and
$z$.  The complete compact polynomial-line theorem of
\cite{LongPolynomialKZ} gives, after its canonical linear reduction, a
polynomial algebra over a finite exponential group algebra.  In particular,
$\Rcal_\partial(\mathscr C)$ is a normal noetherian domain.  Put
\[
  K_\partial(\mathscr C)=\Frac\Rcal_\partial(\mathscr C).
\]
This is the correct coefficient field: every endpoint forcing term is
actually represented in the formal boundary category.

\begin{theorem}[Endpoint-complete functional polynomial comparison]
\label{thm:intro-endpoint-functional}
Let $\mathscr C$ be endpoint-complete Stokes--Lefschetz.  Over
$K_\partial(\mathscr C)$ the full polynomial relation ideal among the flag
functions and a finite first-order differential closure is generated by:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.4em]
\item the completed linear relations coming from algebraic pullback and
      trace, phase-horizontal polynomial Stokes, balanced faces, punctual
      endpoint terms, and relative Jacobian correspondences;
\item the determinant-character binomials of the joint central quotient of
      the primitive differential Galois blocks.
\end{enumerate}
There are no other functional polynomial relations.
\end{theorem}

The determinant clause is explicit.  After removing the translation-active
columns and all constant column relations, a primitive block of rank $n$
with fewer than $n$ split columns has dense affine orbit and contributes no
relation.  A square full-rank block contributes its determinant coordinate.
Across all square blocks, let $T_{\det}\subset\Gm^s$ be the image of the
homogeneous differential Galois group under the determinant characters and
put
\[
  \Lambda_{\det}
  =\{a\in\Z^s:\chi^a|_{T_{\det}}=1\}.
\]
The residual ideal is generated by
\[
  \delta^{a^+}-\eta_a\delta^{a^-},
  \qquad a\in\Lambda_{\det},
\]
where $\eta_a\in K_\partial(\mathscr C)^\times$.  Clearing a boundary
denominator writes each generator entirely in the formal compact boundary
algebra.

\begin{theorem}[Endpoint-complete numerical polynomial theorem]
\label{thm:intro-endpoint-value}
Let $\mathscr C$ be endpoint-complete Stokes--Lefschetz and let
$\xi\in k^\times$ be nonsingular for a common $E$-function system.  Every
polynomial relation over $k$ among the values
\[
  F_{D,q,\Omega}(\xi),
  \qquad (D,q,\Omega)\in\mathscr C,
\]
is generated by specialization at $z=\xi$ of the operations in
\cref{thm:intro-endpoint-functional}, multiplication and Fubini, and the
complete compact polynomial-line theorem.  Equivalently, the endpoint-complete
formal period map defined in \cref{def:endpoint-formal-algebra} is injective.
\end{theorem}

The specialization step is local and denominator-free.  The polynomial-line
value theorem implies that the evaluation prime of the boundary algebra is
exactly $(z-\xi)$.  Its localization is therefore a discrete valuation ring.
The affine orbit quotient is flat over that ring: linear and translation
coordinates are polynomial, the determinant map is flat, and the residual
lattice quotient is free because all partial-character coefficients are
units.  Hence a functional relation regular at $\xi$ contracts to the local
formal ideal.  Beukers lifting supplies precisely such a functional relation
with the prescribed numerical specialization.

\begin{corollary}[One endpoint-complete flag layer]
\label{cor:intro-one-layer}
For one endpoint-complete flag datum, every numerical polynomial relation at
a nonsingular nonzero algebraic parameter is generated by completed twisted
Stokes and the compact polynomial-line boundary theorem.  No determinant
binomial occurs.
\end{corollary}

Indeed, a nonzero rational weight-one block has even rank $n\ge2$, while one
flag layer supplies only one affine solution column.  After any splitting,
its column rank is at most one and hence strictly smaller than $n$; the
special-linear orbit is dense.  If the primitive block is zero, the original
denominator-free relative Stokes theorem already reduces the period to the
faces.

\section{Polynomial flags and phase densities}\label{sec:flags}

\subsection{Polynomial flag chains}

\begin{definition}[Polynomial two-chain]\label{def:poly-chain}
A polynomial two-chain over $k_{\R}$ is a finite $k$-linear combination of
chains
\[
  D=\Phi_*[0,1]^2,
  \qquad
  \Phi\in k_{\R}[u,v]^2.
\]
Its boundary is the alternating sum of the four polynomial face chains.
A polynomial flag chain is a polynomial two-chain for which, after an affine
change of the square coordinates,
\[
  \Phi(u,v)=\bigl(X(u),A(u)+vB(u)\bigr)
\]
with $X,A,B\in k_{\R}[u]$.
\end{definition}

The chain language is preferable to set-theoretic domains: it records
orientation, multiplicity, and algebraic change of variables without
requiring injectivity of $\Phi$.

For a polynomial two-form $\Omega$, define
\[
  F_{D,q,\Omega}(z)=\int_D e^{zq}\Omega.
\]
Pulling back to the square writes this as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square-form}
  F_{D,q,\Omega}(z)
  =\int_0^1\int_0^1P(u,v)e^{zQ(u,v)}\,du\,dv,
  \qquad P,Q\in k[u,v].
\end{equation}

\subsection{Gelfand--Leray density}

Let $q\in k_{\R}[x,y]$ and let $D$ be a real polynomial flag chain.  Put
$T=q(|D|)\subset\R$.  Remove the critical values of $q$, the critical values
of the restrictions of $q$ to the faces, and the phase values of the face
vertices.  The complement is a finite union of open intervals $e$.

For $t\in e$, let
\[
  C_t=q^{-1}(t),
  \qquad
  B_t=C_t\cap|\partial D|,
  \qquad
  \gamma_t=D\cap C_t.
\]
The last object is an oriented relative one-cycle in $(C_t,B_t)$, with the
multiplicities inherited from $D$.

Let $\Omega$ be a polynomial two-form.  On the smooth locus of $q$, choose a
one-form $\alpha$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GL}
  dq\wedge\alpha=\Omega.
\end{equation}
The restriction class of $\alpha$ to $C_t$ is independent of the choice.
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density}
  \rho_e(t)=\int_{\gamma_t}\alpha_t.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Phase-density formula]\label{lem:density-formula}
After discarding chain components on which the pullback of $\Omega$ is zero,
there is an $L^1$ density $\rho$ on the compact set $T=q(|D|)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:laplace-density}
  F_{D,q,\Omega}(z)=\int_T e^{zt}\rho(t)\,dt.
\end{equation}
On every regular interval $e$, the restriction $\rho_e$ is real analytic and
is a branch of a period of the algebraic Gauss--Manin system.  In particular,
it admits multivalued holomorphic continuation on the regular phase line.  No
claim that $\rho_e$ is an algebraic function is made.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On a compact subinterval of $e$, the map $q$ is a submersion on the sliced
chain.  The Gelfand--Leray or coarea formula gives
\[
  \int_{D\cap q^{-1}(e)}e^{zq}\Omega
  =\int_e e^{zt}\left(\int_{\gamma_t}\alpha_t\right)dt.
\]
The inner integral is a period of the relative algebraic de Rham class
$[\alpha_t]$ and therefore is real analytic on $e$ and has the usual
multivalued Gauss--Manin continuation.  The coarea decomposition of the
finite measure obtained by integrating the polynomial density on the compact
chain shows that the resulting density is integrable across the finitely many
critical and boundary values.  Summing the regular intervals proves
\eqref{eq:laplace-density}.  A full-dimensional component on which $q$ is
constant cannot occur for nonconstant polynomial $q$; a lower-dimensional
component pulls every two-form back to zero.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Laplace uniqueness}

\begin{lemma}[Compact-support Laplace uniqueness]\label{lem:laplace-unique}
Let $\rho$ be a compactly supported integrable function on $\R$.  If
\[
  \int_{\R}e^{zt}\rho(t)\,dt=0
  \qquad(z\in\C),
\]
then $\rho=0$ almost everywhere.  If $\rho$ is analytic on each member of a
finite interval decomposition, it vanishes identically on every open
interval.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Restriction to $z=is$, $s\in\R$, is the Fourier transform of the compactly
supported $L^1$ function $\rho$.  Fourier uniqueness gives almost-everywhere
vanishing, and analyticity gives intervalwise vanishing.
\end{proof}

For multiphase families it is useful to retain the Cauchy-transform form.

\begin{lemma}[Cauchy jump separation]\label{lem:Cauchy-jump}
Let $T\subset\R$ be a finite union of compact intervals and let $\rho_j$ be
piecewise analytic integrable densities on $T$.  If
\[
  \sum_j c_j\int_T e^{zt}\rho_j(t)\,dt=0
  \qquad(z\in\C),
\]
then
\[
  \sum_j c_j\rho_j(t)=0
\]
on every open regular interval.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
All moments of the combined density vanish.  Its Cauchy transform
\[
  \mathcal C(s)=\int_T\frac{\sum_j c_j\rho_j(t)}{s-t}\,dt
\]
therefore vanishes for large $s$ and hence on $\C\setminus T$.  At an interior
point of a regular interval, the two lateral values differ by $2\pi i$ times
the analytic density.  The jump is zero, so the density vanishes.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why real phase support is imposed]\label{rem:phase-support}
For a general complex-valued polynomial phase on a real two-chain, the
pushforward is a two-dimensional measure in the complex phase plane.  Its
holomorphic moments can all vanish without the measure vanishing.  Thus the
one-variable Cauchy jump argument is unavailable.  The phase-fibered
hypothesis is mathematical content, not a notational convenience.
\end{remark}

\section{Polynomial-flag periods are \texorpdfstring{$E$}{E}-functions}\label{sec:E-functions}

We use Beukers' normalization of Siegel $E$-functions.  An entire function
\[
  F(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_n\frac{z^n}{n!}
\]
is an $E$-function if $a_n\in k$, the joint logarithmic height of the first
$n$ coefficients is $O(n)$, and $F$ satisfies a nonzero linear differential
equation over $k(z)$.

\begin{theorem}[Polynomial-flag $E$-function theorem]\label{thm:E-function}
For $P,Q\in k[u,v]$, the function
\[
  F(z)=\int_0^1\int_0^1P(u,v)e^{zQ(u,v)}\,du\,dv
\]
is an $E$-function.  Consequently every polynomial-flag period
$F_{D,q,\Omega}$ is an $E$-function.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Termwise integration gives
\[
  F(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_n\frac{z^n}{n!},
  \qquad
  a_n=\int_0^1\int_0^1P(u,v)Q(u,v)^n\,du\,dv.
\]
Choose a number field $K$ containing the coefficients and an integer $d\ge1$
such that $dP,dQ$ have algebraic-integer coefficients.  The degree of
$PQ^n$ is $O(n)$, the number of its monomials is polynomial in $n$, and every
archimedean conjugate of every coefficient is bounded by $C^n$ for a fixed
$C$.  Writing
\[
  PQ^n=\sum_{i,j} c_{i,j,n}u^iv^j
\]
gives
\[
  a_n=\sum_{i,j}\frac{c_{i,j,n}}{(i+1)(j+1)}.
\]
If $N_n=O(n)$ bounds all occurring exponents, then
\[
  d^{n+1}\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,N_n+1)^2
\]
clears all denominators.  The prime number theorem in its elementary
Chebyshev form gives
$\log\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,N)=O(N)$; hence the common denominator has
logarithm $O(n)$.  The conjugate and monomial-count estimates give the same
bound for the joint height.

The integrand $P(u,v)e^{zQ(u,v)}$ is holonomic in $(u,v,z)$.  Holonomic
$D$-modules are stable under algebraic direct image, and integration over the
fixed relative chain gives a holonomic function of $z$; see
\cite[Chapters~1 and~3]{HTT}.  Therefore $F$ satisfies a nonzero differential
equation over $k(z)$.  The integral is entire by uniform convergence on
compact subsets.  All three $E$-function conditions hold.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Beukers lifting]\label{thm:Beukers}
Let $F_1,\ldots,F_N$ be $E$-functions forming a vector solution of a
first-order system over $k(z)$, and let $\xi\in k^\times$ be a nonsingular
point.  Every homogeneous polynomial relation of degree $d$ among the values
$F_i(\xi)$ lifts to a polynomial-coefficient functional relation, homogeneous
of the same degree, specializing to the prescribed relation at $z=\xi$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is the combination of \cite[Theorems~1.3 and~3.2]{Beukers}; affine
relations are homogenized by adjoining the constant $E$-function $1$.
\end{proof}

\section{Relative affine-Galois linearity}\label{sec:relative-AG}

\begin{theorem}[Kolchin--Ostrowski]\label{thm:KO}
Let $K$ be a differential field of characteristic zero with algebraically
closed constants $C$.  Suppose $Y_1,\ldots,Y_m$ lie in a no-new-constants
differential extension and satisfy $DY_i\in K$.  They are algebraically
dependent over $K$ if and only if some nonzero constant linear combination of
them belongs to $K$.
\end{theorem}

This is the classical antiderivative theorem; see
\cite{Kolchin,vanDerPutSinger}.

Let
\[
  R_Y=\{c\in C^m:c\cdot\mathbf Y\in K\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Affine-linear presentation]\label{thm:relative-AG}
Let $r=\dim_C R_Y$.  There is $S\in\operatorname{GL}_m(C)$ such that, for
$\mathbf Z=S\mathbf Y$,
\[
  Z_i=g_i\in K
  \quad(1\le i\le r),
\]
and $Z_{r+1},\ldots,Z_m$ are algebraically independent over $K$.  Hence
\[
  \ker\bigl(K[\mathbf X]\to K[\mathbf Y]\bigr)
  =(Z_1-g_1,\ldots,Z_r-g_r).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose a basis of $R_Y$ and extend it to a basis of $C^m$; use these row
vectors to form $S$.  The first $r$ transformed coordinates lie in $K$.  If
the remaining coordinates were algebraically dependent, \cref{thm:KO}
would put a nonzero constant linear combination of them in $K$, contradicting
the choice of complement.  Division by the monic linear polynomials
$Z_i-g_i$ leaves a polynomial in algebraically independent variables, proving
the kernel statement.
\end{proof}

\subsection{One iterated layer}

Let $x$ be an outer variable and let $K$ be a differential field containing
the inner period functions and their $x$-derivatives.  If
\[
  V_i(x,z)=\int_{x_0}^x f_i(t,z)\,dt,
  \qquad f_i\in K,
\]
then \cref{thm:relative-AG} describes the complete polynomial relation ideal
among the partial outer integrals over $K$.

\begin{remark}[Endpoint evaluation is a separate problem]
\label{rem:endpoint-warning}
Take algebraically independent antiderivatives
\[
  V_i(x)=\int_b^x f_i(t)\,dt
\]
over a differential field $K$.  They have no functional algebraic relation
over $K$, but $V_1(b)=\cdots=V_m(b)=0$.  Endpoint evaluation therefore
creates relations not present in the variable-endpoint function field.  In
particular, \cref{thm:relative-AG} cannot by itself justify a value-level
polynomial theorem for completed two-variable periods.
\end{remark}

\section{The face lattice and the generalized Jacobian}\label{sec:face}

Let $\bar f:\bar{\mathcal C}\to S$ be a smooth proper family of connected
curves and let $\pi:B\to S$ be a nonempty reduced divisor finite \'etale over $S$.
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:face-lattice}
  \Fcal_B
  =\ker\left(\pi_*\Z_B\xrightarrow{\deg}\Z_S\right).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Relative homology sequence]\label{prop:relative-homology}
There is a natural exact sequence of integral local systems
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-homology-seq}
  0\longrightarrow R_1\bar f_*\Z
  \longrightarrow R_1\bar f_*(\bar{\mathcal C},B;\Z)
  \xrightarrow{\partial}\Fcal_B
  \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply the homology long exact sequence of the pair $(\bar C_s,B_s)$.
Connectedness gives $H_0(\bar C_s,\Z)=\Z$, and the map
$H_0(B_s,\Z)\to H_0(\bar C_s,\Z)$ is the degree map.  The construction is
locally constant in $s$ and therefore yields \eqref{eq:relative-homology-seq}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Generalized-Jacobian torus]\label{prop:gen-Jac}
Let $J=\Pic^0(\bar{\mathcal C}/S)$ and
$J_B=\Pic^0(\bar{\mathcal C}/S,B)$.  There is an exact sequence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gen-Jac-seq}
  1\longrightarrow T_B\longrightarrow J_B\longrightarrow J\longrightarrow0,
  \qquad
  T_B=\Res_{B/S}\Gm/\Gm,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:character-face}
  X^*(T_B)\simeq\Fcal_B.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For reduced modulus, the generalized Jacobian is the extension of the ordinary
Jacobian by the quotient of the Weil restriction of $\Gm$ by the diagonal
$\Gm$; see \cite[Chapter~V]{SerreAGCF}.  Characters are contravariant.  The
character sequence associated to
\[
  1\to\Gm\to\Res_{B/S}\Gm\to T_B\to1
\]
is
\[
  0\to X^*(T_B)\to\pi_*\Z_B\xrightarrow{\deg}\Z_S\to0,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:character-face}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Variance and weights]\label{rem:variance}
The relative-homology quotient $\Fcal_B$ is a lattice of boundary classes.
The same lattice is the character group of the torus $T_B$; the torus itself
is dual.  In mixed-Hodge terms, relative homology and punctured-curve
cohomology carry opposite Tate twists.  The slogan ``the face module is the
toric part'' is correct only after this variance is specified.
\end{remark}

For a polynomial flag slice, $B_t$ is the finite set of intersections with
the oriented faces and
\[
  \partial\gamma_t\in\Fcal_{B,t}.
\]
The equation $\deg\partial\gamma_t=0$ is simultaneously the balanced face
relation and the boundary-of-boundary identity.

\section{Rooted Picard--Lefschetz observability}\label{sec:PL}

Let $H$ be a finite-dimensional vector space and suppose the monodromy group
$G$ is generated by relative Picard--Lefschetz transformations
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-PL}
  (T_i-I)\xi
  =\varepsilon_i\langle\widetilde\nu_i,\xi\rangle\nu_i,
  \qquad \varepsilon_i\in\{\pm1\}.
\end{equation}
Here $\nu_i$ and $\widetilde\nu_i$ are vanishing and dual vanishing cycles.
We use the standard relative Picard--Lefschetz formula; see, for example,
\cite[Chapters~2--3]{DimcaSingularities}.
Let $\gamma$ be a relative slice class.

\begin{definition}[Rooted variation graph]\label{def:rooted-graph}
Put
\[
  I_\gamma
  =\{i:\langle\widetilde\nu_i,\gamma\rangle\ne0\}.
\]
Put a directed edge $i\to j$ when
\[
  \langle\widetilde\nu_j,\nu_i\rangle\ne0.
\]
Let $R_\gamma$ be the vertices reachable from $I_\gamma$, and set
\[
  \Vcal_\gamma=\Span\{\nu_i:i\in R_\gamma\},
  \qquad
  \Ocal_\gamma=\Span\{g\gamma-\gamma:g\in G\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Exact rooted orbit theorem]\label{thm:rooted-orbit}
One has
\[
  \Ocal_\gamma=\Vcal_\gamma.
\]
The space is invariant under $G$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The space $\Ocal_\gamma$ is invariant because
\[
  h(g\gamma-\gamma)
  =(hg\gamma-\gamma)-(h\gamma-\gamma).
\]
If $i\in I_\gamma$, \eqref{eq:relative-PL} gives $\nu_i\in\Ocal_\gamma$.
If $\nu_i\in\Ocal_\gamma$ and $i\to j$, then
\[
  (T_j-I)\nu_i
  =\varepsilon_j\langle\widetilde\nu_j,\nu_i\rangle\nu_j,
\]
so $\nu_j\in\Ocal_\gamma$.  Hence
$\Vcal_\gamma\subseteq\Ocal_\gamma$.  The same formula shows that every
$T_j$ preserves $\Vcal_\gamma$.

Conversely, for every generator,
\[
  T_j\gamma-\gamma\in\Vcal_\gamma.
\]
If $g\gamma-\gamma\in\Vcal_\gamma$, then
\[
  T_j g\gamma-\gamma
  =T_j(g\gamma-\gamma)+(T_j\gamma-\gamma)
  \in\Vcal_\gamma.
\]
The same argument applies to $T_j^{-1}$, since
$T_j^{-1}-I$ has the same rank-one image.  Induction on word length in the
generators and their inverses gives
$g\gamma-\gamma\in\Vcal_\gamma$ for every $g$, proving the reverse
inclusion.
\end{proof}

On absolute curve homology the intersection graph is undirected.

\begin{corollary}[Connected-graph observability]\label{cor:connected-observable}
Suppose the vanishing cycles span $V$, their intersection graph is connected,
and $I_\gamma\ne\varnothing$.  Then
\[
  \Ocal_\gamma=V.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Every vertex is reachable from the nonempty active set.  Apply
\cref{thm:rooted-orbit}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Absolute irreducibility]\label{prop:absolute-irred}
Assume in addition that $V$ carries a nondegenerate alternating pairing and
that the $T_i$ are the associated symplectic transvections.  Under the
hypotheses of \cref{cor:connected-observable}, the representation on $V$ is
absolutely irreducible.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $K/\Q$ be a characteristic-zero extension and let
$0\ne W\subset V_K$ be invariant.  Choose $0\ne w\in W$.  Since the
vanishing cycles span and the pairing is nondegenerate, some
$\langle w,\nu_i\rangle$ is nonzero.  Then
$(T_i-I)w$ is a nonzero multiple of $\nu_i$, so $\nu_i\in W$.
Connectivity propagates membership to every vanishing cycle.  Thus
$W=V_K$.
\end{proof}

\section{Denominator-free relative exactness}\label{sec:relative-exactness}

\subsection{Bonnet's theorem}

For a polynomial map $q:\C^2\to\C$, a polynomial one-form $\omega$ is
\emph{topologically relatively exact} if its restriction to every generic
fibre has zero integral on every loop.  It is \emph{algebraically relatively
exact} if
\[
  \omega=dR+a\,dq,
  \qquad R,a\in\C[x,y].
\]

\begin{lemma}[Bonnet's hypotheses in relative dimension one]
\label{lem:Bonnet-hypotheses}
Let $q:\C^2\to\C$ be nonconstant.  If its critical locus is finite and every
fibre is connected, then $q$ is primitive and quasi-fibered in Bonnet's
terminology.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A finite critical locus has codimension two, so $q$ is nonsingular in
codimension one.  Since the target has dimension one, Bonnet's requirement
that the fibres be $1$-generically connected means that every fibre is
connected.  His $2$-generic nonemptiness condition is automatic: for every
$c\in\C$, the proper ideal $(q-c)\subset\C[x,y]$ has a zero by the
Nullstellensatz.  The weaker generic connectedness and nonemptiness conditions
defining primitivity follow as well.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bonnet--Gavrilov relative exactness]\label{thm:Bonnet}
If $q:\C^2\to\C$ has finite critical locus and every fibre is connected, then
every topologically relatively exact polynomial one-form is algebraically
relatively exact.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{lem:Bonnet-hypotheses} and
\cite[Theorem~1.5]{BonnetRelativeExactness}.  Bonnet also records the earlier
two-variable connected-and-reduced theorem of Gavrilov.
\end{proof}

\subsection{From a zero phase density to relative exactness}

Let $\Omega$ be a polynomial two-form.  Choose a polynomial one-form $\omega$
with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:domega}
  d\omega=\Omega;
\end{equation}
this is possible because $H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(\Atwo)=0$.
On a regular fibre choose $\alpha$ as in \eqref{eq:GL}.  The
Gelfand--Leray formula is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GM-derivative}
  \nabla[\omega_t]=[\alpha_t].
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Observable density kills the Gelfand--Leray class]
\label{lem:kill-alpha}
Let $(D,q,\Omega)$ satisfy \textup{(A2)--(A5)} of
\cref{def:admissible}.  If the phase density vanishes on a regular interval
on which the sliced chain is nonzero and has nonzero Lefschetz incidence, then
\[
  [\alpha_t]=0\in H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(C_t)
\]
for the generic affine fibre.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On the active interval in the hypothesis, the period
$\int_{\gamma_t}\alpha_t$ vanishes and hence, by analytic continuation,
on every continuation of that branch.  Therefore
$[\alpha_t]$ annihilates
\[
  \Span\{g\gamma_t-\gamma_t:g\in G\}.
\]
By \cref{cor:connected-observable}, this is the complete projective
weight-one homology.  The one-place-at-infinity hypothesis identifies this
with $H_1(C_t)$ of the affine fibre.  The de Rham--Betti pairing is perfect,
so $[\alpha_t]=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The primitive one-form is fibrewise exact]\label{lem:omega-exact}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{lem:kill-alpha}, the restriction of $\omega$ to
the generic fibre is exact.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:GM-derivative} and \cref{lem:kill-alpha} show that
$[\omega_t]$ is horizontal.  It is therefore monodromy invariant.  For a
Picard--Lefschetz transvection, invariance of the cohomology class forces its
period on the corresponding vanishing cycle to be zero.  Since the vanishing
cycles span $H_1(C_t)$, all periods of $\omega_t$ vanish.  Hence the
restriction is exact.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Polynomial relative Stokes]\label{thm:polynomial-relative-Stokes}
Let $(D,q,\Omega)$ be admissible observable.  If the phase density vanishes on
every regular interval, there is $a\in k[x,y]$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Omega-a}
  \Omega=da\wedge dq.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twisted-primitive}
  e^{zq}\Omega=d\bigl(e^{zq}a\,dq\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \textup{(A5)} there is an active regular interval with nonzero Lefschetz
incidence.  The density vanishes there by hypothesis, so
\cref{lem:kill-alpha,lem:omega-exact} show that $\omega$ is topologically
relatively exact.  Apply \cref{thm:Bonnet}:
\[
  \omega=dR+a\,dq
\]
with $R,a\in\C[x,y]$.  Taking exterior derivatives and using
\eqref{eq:domega} gives \eqref{eq:Omega-a}.  The coefficients descend to
$k$: after fixing the finitely many monomials occurring in one solution,
coefficient comparison is a finite linear system over $k$.  Finally,
$dq\wedge dq=0$ gives \eqref{eq:twisted-primitive}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:intro-single}]
Functional vanishing and \cref{lem:laplace-unique} make every regular phase
density zero.  Apply \cref{thm:polynomial-relative-Stokes} and ordinary
Stokes:
\[
  F(z)=\int_{\partial D}e^{zq}a\,dq.
\]
Every face is polynomially parametrized, so the right side is a finite
combination of compact polynomial-line functions.  It vanishes identically
because $F\equiv0$.  The functional formal theorem of
\cite{LongLinearKZ} makes this boundary relation formal.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why one place at infinity appears]\label{rem:infinity}
If the generic affine fibre has $r>1$ points at infinity, then
$H_1(C_t)$ contains an additional rank-$r-1$ puncture lattice.  Projective
Picard--Lefschetz observability does not detect it.  One may replace
\textup{(A2)} by an explicit infinity-lattice observability hypothesis, but it
cannot simply be omitted.
\end{remark}

\section{Hodge rigidity and Jacobian correspondences}\label{sec:Hodge}

\begin{theorem}[Deligne uniqueness for irreducible factors]
\label{thm:Deligne-unique}
An irreducible complex local system underlying a polarizable complex
variation of Hodge structure carries such a variation uniquely up to tensoring
by a rank-one complex Hodge structure $\C(r,s)$, equivalently up to a shift of
the Hodge bigrading.
\end{theorem}

This is \cite[Proposition~1.13]{DeligneFinitude}; see also the convenient
formulation in \cite[Theorem~2]{BakkerMatsushita}.

\begin{theorem}[Weight-one Hodge rigidity]\label{thm:Hodge-rigidity}
Let $\mathbb V$ and $\mathbb W$ be nonzero polarizable rational variations of
Hodge structure of effective weight one on a connected smooth algebraic base.
Assume their complex local systems are irreducible.  Every nonzero flat map
\[
  \phi:\mathbb V_{\C}\longrightarrow\mathbb W_{\C}
\]
is a morphism of complex variations of Hodge structure.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Irreducibility makes $\phi$ an isomorphism of complex local systems.  Transport
the Hodge bigrading of $\mathbb W$ to $\mathbb V$.  By
\cref{thm:Deligne-unique}, the transported grading differs from the original
one by a shift $\C(r,s)$.  Because both variations are effective of weight
one and arise from rational Hodge structures, both types $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$
occur.  The shifted set of types can again be exactly
$\{(1,0),(0,1)\}$ only when $r=s=0$.  Thus $\phi$ preserves the Hodge
bigrading and hence the Hodge filtration.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The reducible warning]\label{rem:reducible-Hodge}
The theorem fails for repeated reducible local systems.  A constant elliptic
curve gives a two-dimensional trivial local system; most constant matrices are
flat but do not preserve $H^{1,0}$.  The irreducibility hypothesis is therefore
load-bearing.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Algebraic realization]\label{prop:Jac-correspondence}
Let $f_i:\mathcal C_i\to S$ and $f_j:\mathcal C_j\to S$ be smooth proper
curve families over a smooth connected complex algebraic base, with relative
Jacobians $J_i,J_j$.  A rational morphism of weight-one variations
\[
  R^1f_{i*}\Q\longrightarrow R^1f_{j*}\Q
\]
is induced, after multiplication by a positive integer and with the usual
contravariance convention, by a homomorphism of abelian schemes between
$J_i$ and $J_j$.  Its action on $R^1$ is represented by an algebraic divisor
correspondence on $\mathcal C_i\times_S\mathcal C_j$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Hodge realization functor from abelian schemes over $S$ to torsion-free
polarizable integral variations of types $(-1,0)$ and $(0,-1)$ is fully
faithful; see \cite[Rappel~(4.4.3)]{DeligneHodgeIII}.  After multiplying the
rational morphism by an integer, full faithfulness therefore supplies the
corresponding homomorphism of relative Jacobians.

The homomorphism and the Poincar\'e bundle determine a relative line bundle on
$J_i\times_S J_j$.  Pulling it back along the Abel--Jacobi maps (after an
inessential finite base change if relative degree-one divisor classes are
needed) gives a line bundle on
$\mathcal C_i\times_S\mathcal C_j$.  Its first Chern class, represented by a
relative divisor with rational coefficients after rescaling, acts on $R^1$ as
the original morphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Complex intertwiners are generated by correspondences]
\label{cor:complex-correspondences}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{thm:Hodge-rigidity}, every complex flat
intertwiner is a complex linear combination of rational VHS morphisms and
therefore, by \cref{prop:Jac-correspondence}, of algebraic Jacobian
correspondences.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For rational local systems, the intertwiner space is the kernel of a finite
system of rational linear equations.  Hence scalar extension gives
\[
  \Hom_{\pi_1}(\mathbb V,\mathbb W)\otimes_{\Q}\C
  \simeq
  \Hom_{\pi_1}(\mathbb V_{\C},\mathbb W_{\C}).
\]
Apply \cref{thm:Hodge-rigidity,prop:Jac-correspondence} to the rational
summands.
\end{proof}

\section{Multiphase functional comparison}\label{sec:multiphase}

Let $S$ be the phase line with all critical and boundary branch values removed.
After a common finite \'etale base change, assume that all face points are
sections.  For each datum let
\[
  0\to V_j\to H_j^{\mathrm{rel}}\to\Fcal_j\to0
\]
be the relative-homology sequence and let $\gamma_j$ be the sliced relative
class.  Let $\alpha_j$ denote the Gelfand--Leray cohomology class.

\subsection{The direct-sum variation space}

Set
\[
  V=\bigoplus_{j=1}^N V_j,
  \qquad
  \Gamma=(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_N),
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:combined-orbit}
  \Ocal_\Gamma
  =\Span\{g\Gamma-\Gamma:g\in\pi_1(S)\}\subseteq V.
\end{equation}
The projection of $\Ocal_\Gamma$ to $V_j$ is $\Ocal_{\gamma_j}$ and hence,
for an observable datum, is all of $V_j$.

\begin{lemma}[Disjoint irreducible factors]\label{lem:disjoint-factors}
Let $V_1,\ldots,V_N$ be pairwise nonisomorphic irreducible complex
representations of a group $G$.  If a $G$-submodule
$W\subseteq\bigoplus_j V_j$ projects nontrivially to every $V_j$, then
\[
  W=\bigoplus_j V_j.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The finite-dimensional image algebra of $\C[G]$ is semisimple on the direct
sum.  A submodule is therefore a direct sum of some of the pairwise
nonisomorphic simple factors.  Nonzero projection to each factor forces every
factor to occur.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof in the disjoint-block case}

\begin{theorem}[Disjoint-block functional comparison]
\label{thm:disjoint-comparison}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{thm:intro-multiphase}, every
constant-coefficient functional relation is generated by the operations
listed there.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:Cauchy-jump}, the combined phase density vanishes on every open
regular interval.  Fix such an interval $e$ and let $J_e$ be the indices for
which the sliced chain is nonzero on $e$.  Analytic continuation to a base
point of the common regular phase line gives, for every $g\in\pi_1(S)$,
\[
  \sum_{j\in J_e}c_j\langle\alpha_j,g\gamma_{j,e}\rangle=0.
\]
Subtract the equation for $g=1$.  The covector
\[
  \alpha_e=(c_j\alpha_j)_{j\in J_e}
  \in\left(\bigoplus_{j\in J_e}V_j\right)^\vee
\]
annihilates the orbit-variation submodule associated with the tuple
$(\gamma_{j,e})_{j\in J_e}$.  By admissibility, its projection to every
$V_j$, $j\in J_e$, is surjective.  The factors remain pairwise
nonisomorphic, so \cref{lem:disjoint-factors} makes the orbit submodule the
whole direct sum.  Hence $c_j\alpha_j=0$ for every $j\in J_e$.

Every nonzero two-chain term is active on at least one regular interval.
Repeating the argument over the finite interval decomposition therefore gives
$c_j\alpha_j=0$ for every index with $c_j\ne0$.  The proof of
\cref{thm:polynomial-relative-Stokes} then applies to the scaled form
$c_j\Omega_j$ and gives a polynomial $a_j$ with
\[
  c_j\Omega_j=da_j\wedge dq_j.
\]
Stokes reduces the relation to the faces.  The face terms are compact
polynomial-line functional relations; they are generated by the formal
operations of \cite{LongLinearKZ}.  Their zero-dimensional endpoint terms
are already part of the compact polynomial-line formal space.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Isomorphic blocks}

Hodge rigidity makes every flat isomorphism between absolutely irreducible
effective weight-one blocks Hodge-theoretic and hence algebraic on the relative
Jacobians.  A second condition is needed for
the relative cycles.

\begin{definition}[Relative correspondence compatibility]
\label{def:relative-compatible}
Fix an isotypic collection with common absolutely irreducible factor $V$, and
use algebraic Jacobian correspondences to identify its copies of $V$.  For a
coefficient vector $m=(m_i)$, put
\[
  c_m(g)=\sum_i m_i\bigl(g\gamma_i-\gamma_i\bigr)\in V.
\]
The collection is \emph{relatively correspondence-compatible} if every
$m$ with $c_m=0$ has the following geometric realization: after a finite base
change and after applying a rational linear combination of algebraic
correspondences to the $\gamma_i$, the relative cycle
$\sum_i m_i\gamma_i$ differs from zero by a class in the balanced face
lattice.  The condition is imposed separately on every isotypic factor.
\end{definition}

This condition is automatic when the slice cycles are literally identified by
an algebraic change of variables, and when several amplitudes are placed on
the same phase and the same slice.  It is not a formal consequence of Hodge
rigidity: a relative cycle is a normal-function datum, not only an absolute
weight-one class.

\begin{theorem}[Isotypic extension]\label{thm:isotypic-extension}
The conclusion of \cref{thm:disjoint-comparison} remains true when pairwise
nonisomorphism is replaced by relative correspondence compatibility.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Decompose the direct sum into isotypic pieces
$V_\lambda\otimes M_\lambda$.  Absolute irreducibility gives
$\End_G(V_\lambda)=\C$, so every submodule is
$V_\lambda\otimes M_\lambda^0$ for a subspace
$M_\lambda^0\subseteq M_\lambda$.  By \cref{cor:complex-correspondences}, every flat intertwiner between two
copies is a complex linear combination of algebraic Jacobian correspondences.  Relative correspondence
compatibility permits the corresponding linear operations on the slice
variation cocycles modulo the face lattices.

Choose a basis in which the orbit-variation submodule occupies the first
multiplicity coordinates.  The combined de Rham covector annihilates those
coordinates, so their cohomology classes are fibrewise zero and
\cref{thm:polynomial-relative-Stokes} applies.  The remaining transformed
relative cycles have zero variation and, by the compatibility hypothesis, are
represented modulo algebraic correspondences by face classes.  The resulting
relations are therefore generated by correspondences, phase-horizontal
Stokes, and the boundary line theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The remaining normal-function issue]\label{rem:normal-function}
Without \cref{def:relative-compatible}, an invariant linear combination of
relative slice classes can define a nontrivial admissible normal function even
when the absolute local system has no fixed part.  Hodge rigidity of the
absolute $H^1$ does not eliminate this possibility.  This is the precise
remaining obstruction in the unrestricted isomorphic-block case.
\end{remark}

\section{A functional formal-period statement}\label{sec:formal}

Fix a finite collection $\mathscr C$ of admissible observable flag data.  Let
$\Pcal^{\mathrm{fun}}_{\mathrm{flag}}(\mathscr C)$ be the $k$-vector space
generated by the symbols
\[
  [D,q,\Omega],\qquad (D,q,\Omega)\in\mathscr C,
\]
together with the compact polynomial-line boundary symbols produced from the
faces, modulo the following explicitly permitted relations:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(F\arabic*)},leftmargin=3.2em]
\item linearity in chains and differential forms;
\item phase-preserving algebraic pullback and trace through auxiliary
      generically finite maps;
\item the phase-horizontal Stokes identities
      \[
        [D,q,da\wedge dq]=[\partial D,q,a\,dq],
        \qquad a\in k[x,y];
      \]
\item the compact polynomial-line functional relations, including their
      zero-dimensional exponential boundary symbols, of
      \cite{LongLinearKZ};
\item when $\mathscr C$ is relatively correspondence-compatible, the
      algebraic Jacobian-correspondence relations appearing in
      \cref{def:relative-compatible}.
\end{enumerate}
The functional period map is
\[
  \per_{\mathrm{fun},\mathscr C}:
  \Pcal^{\mathrm{fun}}_{\mathrm{flag}}(\mathscr C)
  \longrightarrow\Ocal(\C),
  \qquad
  [D,q,\Omega]\longmapsto
  \left(z\mapsto\int_D e^{zq}\Omega\right).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Functional Kontsevich--Zagier theorem on the observable locus]
\label{thm:functional-KZ}
The map $\per_{\mathrm{fun},\mathscr C}$ is injective if the weight-one blocks
of $\mathscr C$ are pairwise nonisomorphic.  It is also injective if
$\mathscr C$ is relatively correspondence-compatible.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is \cref{thm:disjoint-comparison,thm:isotypic-extension}, applied on
each interval of the common regular phase decomposition and then summed.
Every defining relation has zero functional period by algebraic change of
variables, trace, correspondence functoriality, or Stokes, so the map is well
defined.
\end{proof}

\section{Completed phase distributions and the boundary field}
\label{sec:completed-phase}

The functional theorem of \cref{thm:functional-KZ} uses only the ordinary
Laplace transform of the phase density.  Polynomial coefficients in $z$
force us to retain the action of the phase Weyl algebra.  This section makes
that completion precise and identifies every new boundary constituent with
an object already covered by the compact polynomial-line theorem.

\subsection{The compact phase distribution}

Let $(D,q,\Omega)$ be a polynomial flag datum.  Define a compactly supported
distribution on the real phase line by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase-distribution}
  \langle\Theta_{D,q,\Omega},\varphi\rangle
  =\int_D\varphi(q)\Omega,
  \qquad \varphi\in C^\infty(\R).
\end{equation}
Its support is contained in the compact interval $q(|D|)$.  On the regular
intervals of \cref{sec:flags}, it is represented by the analytic
Gelfand--Leray density $\rho_e(t)\,dt$.

For a compactly supported distribution $T$, put
\[
  \mathcal L(T)(z)=\langle T(t),e^{zt}\rangle.
\]
This is an entire function.  With the convention
$\langle\partial_tT,\varphi\rangle=-\langle T,\varphi'\rangle$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Weyl-Laplace}
  \mathcal L(tT)=\partial_z\mathcal L(T),
  \qquad
  \mathcal L(\partial_tT)=-z\mathcal L(T).
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase-Laplace}
  F_{D,q,\Omega}(z)=\mathcal L(\Theta_{D,q,\Omega})(z).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Tensor and convolution compatibility]
\label{lem:tensor-convolution}
If $T_1,\ldots,T_m$ are compactly supported distributions on $\R$, then
\[
  \prod_{j=1}^m\mathcal L(T_j)
  =\mathcal L(T_1*\cdots*T_m).
\]
Moreover, the identities \eqref{eq:Weyl-Laplace} commute with tensor product
and convolution.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The external tensor product is compactly supported.  Push it forward by the
addition map $(t_1,\ldots,t_m)\mapsto t_1+\cdots+t_m$.  Evaluation on
$e^{z(t_1+\cdots+t_m)}$ factors into the product of the evaluations.  The
Weyl identities follow from the distributional Leibniz rule and functoriality
of pushforward.
\end{proof}

The point of using distributions rather than repeatedly integrating by parts
is that no endpoint term is discarded.  The derivative of a piecewise
Gauss--Manin density has three kinds of constituents: the middle-extension
Gauss--Manin part on the punctured phase line, its singular germs at the
critical values, and punctual subquotients supported at critical or boundary
values.  The punctual terms are completely elementary.

\begin{lemma}[Punctual Fourier--Laplace terms]
\label{lem:punctual-Laplace}
A distribution supported at an algebraic point $a$ is a finite sum
$\sum_{m=0}^N c_m\delta_a^{(m)}$.  Its Laplace transform is
\[
  \sum_{m=0}^N c_m(-z)^m e^{az}.
\]
Every such function lies in the differential algebra generated by the
zero-dimensional exponential period $e^{az}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The structure theorem for point-supported distributions gives the first
assertion.  The second follows directly from
$\langle\delta_a^{(m)},e^{zt}\rangle=(-z)^m e^{az}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The completed relative Gauss--Manin module}

Let $j:S\hookrightarrow\Aone_t$ be the complement of the critical values,
face branch values, and endpoint phase values.  Over $S$, the relative
homology sequence of \cref{prop:relative-homology} and the Gelfand--Leray
connection give a regular algebraic connection.  Let $\Mcal^{\mathrm{mid}}$
be the direct sum of the middle extensions of its primitive weight-one
constituents.  Let $\Pcal_a$ denote the finite regular holonomic punctual
module generated by all local cohomology classes of the completed flag direct
image at $a$.

\begin{definition}[Completed phase module]
\label{def:completed-phase-module}
The \emph{completed phase module} $\Mcal^{\mathrm{cmp}}_{D,q,\Omega}$ is the
smallest regular holonomic $D_{\Aone_t}$-subquotient of the algebraic direct
image of the flag datum which contains the distribution class
$\Theta_{D,q,\Omega}$, the primitive middle extension
$\Mcal^{\mathrm{mid}}$, the balanced face module, and every punctual module
$\Pcal_a$ occurring at a critical or boundary phase value.
\end{definition}

The definition is intrinsic: it is the subquotient generated by the actual
relative current, not an arbitrarily enlarged Picard--Vessiot system.

\begin{proposition}[Finite completed closure]
\label{prop:finite-completed-closure}
For a polynomial flag datum, $\Mcal^{\mathrm{cmp}}_{D,q,\Omega}$ is regular
holonomic and has finite rank away from finitely many phase values.  Its
Fourier--Laplace transform is holonomic.  Under \textup{(E2)}, its nonpunctual
primitive part is the Fourier transform of a regular middle-extension module
which is regular at infinity, and every remaining composition factor is a
balanced face factor or a punctual factor of
\cref{lem:punctual-Laplace}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The exponential-free phase direct image is the algebraic direct image of a
regular holonomic relative de Rham complex with a constructible relative
chain condition.  Regular holonomic modules are stable under algebraic direct
image, subquotients, middle extension, and local cohomology.  The chain has
only finitely many critical, branch, and endpoint values, so only finitely
many punctual factors occur.  Fourier--Laplace transformation preserves
holonomicity.  Condition \textup{(E2)} removes an additional weight-one
constituent at infinity and identifies the nonpunctual primitive factor with
the stated middle extension.  See \cite{HTT,DHMS} for the regular-holonomic
and Fourier--Laplace formalism.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Endpoint completion is algebraic]
\label{cor:endpoint-completion-algebraic}
A finite differential closure of the flag functions can be chosen so that
its inhomogeneous forcing coordinates are finite sums of compact
polynomial-line face functions and functions $z^m e^{az}$ with $a\in k$.
No analytic endpoint constant outside the compact polynomial-line category
is introduced.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Fourier--Laplace transformation to
\cref{prop:finite-completed-closure}.  The face factors are direct images of
polynomially parametrized one-chains.  The punctual factors are described by
\cref{lem:punctual-Laplace}.  Choosing cyclic vectors in the finitely many
holonomic factors produces a finite first-order closure with precisely these
forcing terms.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The actual boundary-function algebra}

Fix a finite collection $\mathscr C$.  Let
$\Rcal_\partial(\mathscr C)$ be the $k$-subalgebra of $\Ocal(\C)$ generated
by $z$, by every compact polynomial-line face function occurring in the
completed closures, by the finitely many exponentials $e^{az}$ occurring in
the punctual factors, and by the derivatives needed in the chosen
first-order systems.  We abbreviate it to $\Rcal_\partial$ and put
\[
  K_\partial=\Frac(\Rcal_\partial).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Boundary normal form]
\label{prop:boundary-normal-form}
After quotienting by the complete linear compact polynomial-line relations
and applying an invertible Laurent-polynomial change of the remaining moment
coordinates, there are a finitely generated torsion-free additive group
$\Gamma\subset k$ and algebraically independent coordinates
$T_1,\ldots,T_m$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary-normal-form}
  \Rcal_\partial
  \simeq k[z]\otimes_k k[\Gamma][T_1,\ldots,T_m].
\end{equation}
Consequently $\Rcal_\partial$ is a normal noetherian domain and its field of
constants is $k$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is the finite functional normal form in the complete compact
polynomial-line theorem \cite{LongPolynomialKZ}.  The group $\Gamma$ is a
finitely generated subgroup of the additive group of a characteristic-zero
field, hence is free abelian; its group algebra is a Laurent polynomial
algebra.  Formula \eqref{eq:boundary-normal-form} is therefore a Laurent
polynomial algebra followed by an ordinary polynomial extension, so it is a
normal noetherian domain.  Every generator has Taylor coefficients in $k$.  If a quotient $f/g$ of
two elements of this function field is a complex constant, comparison at the
first nonzero Taylor coefficient of $g$ shows that the constant lies in $k$.
Thus the differential constant field is exactly $k$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Boundary specialization prime]
\label{prop:boundary-specialization-prime}
Let $\xi\in k^\times$ be nonsingular for the finite boundary $E$-function
system.  Evaluation at $z=\xi$ has kernel
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary-evaluation-prime}
  \ker\bigl(\Rcal_\partial\xrightarrow{\operatorname{ev}_\xi}\C\bigr)
  =(z-\xi).
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary-DVR}
  B_{\partial,\xi}
  :=(\Rcal_\partial)_{(z-\xi)}
\end{equation}
is a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer $z-\xi$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The quotient by $(z-\xi)$ is the formal compact polynomial-line value
algebra at $\xi$.  Its numerical period map is injective by the complete
polynomial value theorem of \cite{LongPolynomialKZ}; therefore no further
element vanishes at $\xi$.  The ideal $(z-\xi)$ has height one in the normal
noetherian domain of \cref{prop:boundary-normal-form}.  The localization of
a normal noetherian domain at a height-one prime is a discrete valuation
ring.
\end{proof}

\section{Stokes root generation for the primitive blocks}
\label{sec:Stokes-generation}

We now compute the derived differential Galois group of the primitive
Fourier--Laplace module.  The argument uses only the rank-one Stokes arrows
and a bounded graph condition.

\subsection{Stationary-phase Stokes data}

Let $\Mcal$ be a primitive middle-extension module satisfying
\textup{(E2)}.  Write its distinct finite singular values as
$c_1,\ldots,c_n$.  The vanishing-cycle-basis hypothesis identifies the
stationary-phase solution space of $\widehat\Mcal$ at infinity with
\[
  V=\bigoplus_{i=1}^n k e_i,
\]
where the exponential factor on $ke_i$ is $e^{c_i z}$.  The exponential
torus
\[
  T_{\exp}\subset\GL(V)
\]
acts diagonally, and its character on the root line $kE_{ij}$ is
$\chi_i\chi_j^{-1}$.

The topological Fourier formula of
D'Agnolo--Hien--Morando--Sabbah expresses the two Stokes multipliers in terms
of the quiver of the original regular module.  In the present rank-one
vanishing-cycle situation, every nonzero off-diagonal quiver map supplies a
nonzero root component $E_{ij}$ in a Stokes logarithm; see
\cite[Theorem~5.4]{DHMS}.

\begin{definition}[Stokes root quiver]
\label{def:Stokes-root-quiver}
The Stokes root quiver $Q_{\mathrm{St}}$ has vertices
$1,\ldots,n$ and an arrow $i\to j$ whenever some Stokes logarithm has a
nonzero $E_{ji}$-component.  The indexing convention is immaterial; only the
directed reachability relation is used.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Torus extraction of root lines]
\label{lem:torus-root-extraction}
Let $H\subset\GL(V)$ be an algebraic group containing $T_{\exp}$ and a
unipotent element $u$.  Suppose the nonzero $T_{\exp}$-weights occurring in
$\log u$ are pairwise distinct.  Then $\Lie(H)$ contains every nonzero weight
component of $\log u$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The adjoint orbit of $\log u$ under $T_{\exp}$ lies in $\Lie(H)$.  Its span
is a direct sum of the weight lines occurring in $\log u$.  Because the
characters are distinct, evaluation at finitely many torus points gives a
Vandermonde system which projects onto each weight component.  Hence each
component belongs to $\Lie(H)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Strong root graphs generate special linear groups]
\label{lem:strong-root-SL}
Let $Q$ be a strongly connected directed graph on $\{1,\ldots,n\}$.  If a
Lie subalgebra $\mathfrak h\subset\mathfrak{gl}_n(k)$ contains $E_{ji}$ for
every arrow $i\to j$ of $Q$, then
\[
  \mathfrak{sl}_n(k)\subset\mathfrak h.
\]
Consequently, an algebraic subgroup containing the corresponding root
subgroups contains $\SL_n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a directed path $i=i_0\to i_1\to\cdots\to i_r=j$, repeated use of
\[
  [E_{i_{a+1},i_a},E_{i_{a+2},i_{a+1}}]
  =-E_{i_{a+2},i_a}
\]
produces $E_{ji}$.  Strong connectivity therefore gives every off-diagonal
matrix unit.  Then
$[E_{ij},E_{ji}]=E_{ii}-E_{jj}$ gives the trace-zero diagonal subalgebra.
These matrices span $\mathfrak{sl}_n$.  In characteristic zero, the connected
algebraic subgroup with this Lie algebra contains the simply connected root
group generated by the elementary unipotents, namely $\SL_n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Stokes generation]
\label{thm:Stokes-generation}
For a primitive endpoint-complete Stokes--Lefschetz block of rank $n$, the
differential Galois group $G$ of its Fourier--Laplace module over
$K_\partial$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SL-contained}
  \SL(V)\subset G.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Condition \textup{(E2)} puts the primitive module in the regular-at-infinity
setting of \cite[Theorem~5.4]{DHMS}; its Fourier transform has the linear
exponential factors $c_i z$.  The Stokes formula identifies the nonzero root
components with the arrows of $Q_{\mathrm{St}}$.  The exponential torus is
part of the formal differential Galois group.  Condition \textup{(E3)} and
\cref{lem:torus-root-extraction} put every arrow root line in the Lie algebra
of the analytic differential Galois group.  Strong connectivity and
\cref{lem:strong-root-SL} then give $\mathfrak{sl}(V)$.  Ramis' density
theorem says that formal monodromy, the exponential torus, and the Stokes
subgroups generate a Zariski-dense subgroup of the differential Galois
group over $k(z)$; see \cite[Chapter~8]{vanDerPutSinger}.  Thus that group
contains $\SL(V)$.  On extending the base to $K_\partial$, the new Galois
group is the subgroup corresponding to the Picard--Vessiot intersection with
the differential normal closure of $K_\partial$.  Condition \textup{(E4)}
says that this quotient is central, so the derived subgroup $\SL(V)$
survives the base change.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Several primitive blocks}

Decompose the semisimplification of the primitive homogeneous system as
\[
  M^{\mathrm{prim}}
  =\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}V_\lambda\otimes E_\lambda,
\]
where the $V_\lambda$ are pairwise projectively nonisomorphic simple
modules and $E_\lambda$ are multiplicity spaces after the algebraic
correspondence identifications of \cref{sec:Hodge}.

\begin{proposition}[Product derived group]
\label{prop:product-derived-group}
Under \textup{(E4)}, the derived subgroup of the homogeneous differential
Galois group contains
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product-SL}
  \prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\SL(V_\lambda).
\end{equation}
Any remaining coupling between the blocks factors through their determinant
characters.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:Stokes-generation}, the projection to each simple block contains
$\SL(V_\lambda)$.  A proper subdirect product of simple special-linear
factors can couple two factors only through an isomorphism of their adjoint
simple quotients.  Such an isomorphism identifies the corresponding
projective differential modules, up to the standard--dual outer automorphism
and a rank-one twist.  Condition \textup{(E4)} excludes these identifications
unless they have already been combined by an explicit algebraic
correspondence.  The derived subgroup is therefore the product in
\eqref{eq:product-SL}.  The quotient of each containing subgroup by its
special-linear subgroup is measured by determinant, so every residual
coupling is central and determinant-character valued.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Observable cyclic column]
\label{prop:observable-cyclic-column}
Let $F(z)$ be the scalar flag function of one endpoint-complete datum and
form a cyclic first-order system from $F$ and its derivatives.  Its
projection to a primitive simple block is either zero or is one nonzero
particular-solution column whose homogeneous differential module is the
entire block.  In the zero case, $F$ belongs to the boundary field after the
polynomial relative Stokes reduction.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The differential submodule generated by the primitive projection of the
cyclic vector is either zero or a nonzero submodule of a simple module; in
the latter case it is the whole block.  Derivatives give the coordinates of
that one cyclic column, not additional affine columns.  If the primitive
projection is zero, its Gelfand--Leray class annihilates the rooted monodromy
orbit.  By \cref{thm:rooted-orbit} and the perfect de Rham--Betti pairing it
vanishes on the affine fibre.  The proof of
\cref{thm:polynomial-relative-Stokes} then gives polynomial relative
exactness and reduces $F$ to its compact polynomial-line boundary terms.
\end{proof}

\section{Affine special-linear orbit ideals}
\label{sec:affine-orbits}

We now pass from the homogeneous Stokes group to the complete polynomial
relation ideal of the inhomogeneous flag solutions.  The calculation is
purely algebraic and is included in full.

\begin{lemma}[Reductivity of the primitive homogeneous group]
\label{lem:primitive-reductive}
Every primitive simple block occurring in an endpoint-complete collection has
reductive differential Galois group in its faithful solution representation.
After the correspondence identifications of \textup{(E4)}, the homogeneous
group of the finite direct sum is reductive as well.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $G$ be the differential Galois group of a primitive simple block and let
$U=R_u(G)$.  The connected unipotent group $U$ has a nonzero fixed vector by
Lie--Kolchin.  Because $U$ is normal, its fixed space is $G$-stable; simplicity
of the differential module makes that fixed space the full solution space.
The faithful solution representation therefore kills $U$, so $U=1$ and $G$
is reductive.  A finite product of these groups, enlarged only by the central
determinant quotient allowed in \textup{(E4)}, is again reductive.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Affine Picard--Vessiot form}

Let $(K,D)$ be a differential field of characteristic zero with algebraically
closed constant field $k$.  Let
\[
  Y'=AY,
  \qquad Y\in\GL_n(L),
\]
be a fundamental matrix in a Picard--Vessiot field, and let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inhomogeneous-matrix-system}
  U'=AU+B,
  \qquad U\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times r}(L),
  \qquad B\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times r}(K).
\end{equation}
In the endpoint-complete application, reductivity follows from
\cref{lem:primitive-reductive}.  We state the affine calculation over an
arbitrary reductive homogeneous group.  For a differential automorphism
$\sigma$ one has
\[
  \sigma(Y)=Yg_\sigma,
  \qquad
  \sigma(U)=U+Yc_\sigma.
\]
Thus the affine differential Galois group is a subgroup of
$\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times r}\rtimes G$.

Let $W$ be its translation kernel.  If $G$ contains $\SL(V)$ on the standard
module $V=k^n$, then
\[
  W=V\otimes R
\]
for a uniquely determined subspace $R\subset k^r$: this follows from
$\End_{\SL(V)}(V)=k$.

\begin{lemma}[Translation reduction]
\label{lem:translation-reduction}
After a constant change of the $r$ columns, suppose
$R=\Span(e_1,\ldots,e_t)$.  Then no polynomial relation over $K$ involves
the entries of the first $t$ columns.  Every remaining column is split: there
are $A_0\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times(r-t)}(K)$ and
$C\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times(r-t)}(k)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:split-columns}
  U_{\mathrm{sp}}=A_0+YC.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The translation subgroup adds $Yv$ independently to each of the first $t$
columns.  Since $Y$ is invertible, these translations are Zariski dense in
the corresponding affine coordinate spaces.  A polynomial invariant under
all of them is independent of those entries.  For the remaining columns, projection of the affine translation term to
$V\otimes(k^r/R)$ is a regular algebraic $1$-cocycle of $G$.  Since $G$ is
reductive in characteristic zero, it is linearly reductive and this cocycle
is a coboundary.  Thus there is a constant matrix $C$ such that
$U_{\mathrm{sp}}+YC$ is fixed by the full affine Picard--Vessiot group.  It
therefore equals a matrix $A_0$ over $K$, which is
\eqref{eq:split-columns} after replacing $C$ by $-C$.
\end{proof}

Constant column operations reduce $C$ to a full-column-rank matrix and record
all discarded columns as affine-linear relations over $K$.  We now compute
the remaining orbit.

\begin{lemma}[Special-linear matrix orbits]
\label{lem:SL-matrix-orbits}
Let $C\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times s}(k)$ have column rank $s\le n$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.4em]
\item If $s<n$, the $\SL_n$-orbit of $C$ is the rank-$s$ locus and is Zariski
      dense in $\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times s}$.
\item If $s=n$, the orbit is the hypersurface
      $\det X=\det C$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $s<n$, any two injective maps $k^s\to k^n$ are carried to one another by
an element of $\GL_n$.  The determinant of such an element can be corrected
to one by scaling a complementary basis vector, without changing its action
on the image of $C$.  Hence $\SL_n$ is transitive on the rank-$s$ locus.
That locus is open dense.  For $s=n$, left multiplication by $\SL_n$
preserves determinant, and if $\det X=\det C\ne0$ then
$XC^{-1}\in\SL_n$.  This proves the second assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The joint determinant lattice}

For several primitive blocks, perform \cref{lem:translation-reduction} and
the constant column reduction block by block.  A block is \emph{saturated}
when the number of remaining independent split columns equals its rank.
Only saturated blocks can contribute nonlinear relations.  Enumerate them by
$\lambda=1,\ldots,s$ and write
\[
  \Delta_\lambda
  =\det(U_\lambda-A_\lambda).
\]
Let $G^{\mathrm{hom}}$ be the homogeneous differential Galois group and let
\[
  T_{\det}
  =\overline{\operatorname{im}
     \bigl(G^{\mathrm{hom}}\xrightarrow{(\det_1,\ldots,\det_s)}
     \Gm^s\bigr)}.
\]
Define its character lattice
\begin{equation}\label{eq:det-lattice}
  \Lambda_{\det}
  =\{a\in\Z^s:x^a=1\text{ on }T_{\det}\}.
\end{equation}
The lattice need not be saturated, because $T_{\det}$ may have a finite
component group.

For $a\in\Lambda_{\det}$, the ratio
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta-character}
  \eta_a
  =\frac{\Delta^{a^+}}{\Delta^{a^-}}
\end{equation}
is fixed by the affine differential Galois group and hence belongs to
$K^\times$.

\begin{theorem}[Complete affine orbit ideal]
\label{thm:complete-affine-orbit}
Assume the derived homogeneous group contains the product of the special
linear groups of the pairwise projectively disjoint primitive blocks.  Then
the kernel of the evaluation map from the polynomial ring over $K$ in all
entries of $U$ is generated by:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.4em]
\item the affine-linear relations produced by the translation and constant
      column reductions;
\item for the saturated blocks, the lattice binomials
\begin{equation}\label{eq:det-binomial}
  \Delta^{a^+}-\eta_a\Delta^{a^-},
  \qquad a\in\Lambda_{\det}.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
There are no other polynomial relations.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Translation-active columns contribute a full affine-space factor by
\cref{lem:translation-reduction}.  Affine-linear column relations remove the
constant kernel of each split matrix $C$.  Every unsaturated block then has
full column rank strictly smaller than its row rank, so
\cref{lem:SL-matrix-orbits}(i) makes its orbit closure the full matrix space.

For a saturated block, \cref{lem:SL-matrix-orbits}(ii) says that the special
linear derived group leaves only its determinant coordinate.  After taking
the product over all saturated blocks, the remaining orbit is the translate
of the algebraic subgroup $T_{\det}\subset\Gm^s$ through the base determinant
vector.  The ideal of such a translated subtorus, including a possible
finite component group, is the partial-character lattice ideal generated by
\eqref{eq:det-binomial}; see \cite{EisenbudSturmfels}.  Pulling this ideal
back through the determinant maps and adjoining the already identified
linear relations gives the full orbit-closure ideal.  The Picard--Vessiot
torsor identifies this orbit closure with the algebraic relation variety of
the solution coordinates, proving completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[One-column freeness]
\label{cor:one-column-free}
Let the primitive homogeneous rank be $n\ge2$ and let $U$ consist of one
affine solution column which survives the affine-linear reduction of
\cref{lem:translation-reduction}.  Then the entries of that remaining column
are algebraically independent over $K$.  In particular, it contributes no
nonlinear polynomial relation.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If the translation kernel is nonzero, irreducibility makes it all of $V$, so
translation gives a full affine-space orbit.  If it is zero, the surviving
split column has a nonzero constant column $C$, hence rank one.  Since
$1<n$, \cref{lem:SL-matrix-orbits}(i) gives a dense orbit in $k^n$.  Thus the
remaining coordinate ring is a polynomial ring over $K$ in either case.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Formal determinant-line reduction}

Return to $K=K_\partial$.  Every affine shift $A_\lambda$ and every
$\eta_a$ in \eqref{eq:det-binomial} belongs to the fraction field of the
actual boundary algebra.  Choose
\[
  \eta_a=b_a/c_a,
  \qquad b_a,c_a\in\Rcal_\partial,
  \qquad c_a\ne0.
\]
Then \eqref{eq:det-binomial} is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cleared-det-binomial}
  c_a\Delta^{a^+}-b_a\Delta^{a^-}=0.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Formal determinant-line realization]
\label{prop:formal-det-line}
Relation \eqref{eq:cleared-det-binomial} is a formal tensor relation generated
by exterior powers of the completed differential systems, the horizontal
rank-one determinant-line identifications, multiplication/Fubini, and the
compact polynomial-line boundary theorem.  Different choices of
$b_a/c_a$ give the same formal relation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a saturated block, exterior multiplication of its $n$ split columns gives
\[
  \bigwedge^n(U_\lambda-A_\lambda)
  =(\det C_\lambda)\bigwedge^nY_\lambda.
\]
The right side is a solution of the rank-one determinant connection.  A
character $a\in\Lambda_{\det}$ makes the corresponding tensor product of
determinant lines horizontal and trivial; its horizontal scalar is
$\eta_a$.  Expanding the exterior products gives the determinants
$\Delta_\lambda$, while representing the horizontal scalar in the boundary
fraction field and clearing its denominator gives
\eqref{eq:cleared-det-binomial}.  Exterior expansion is an alternating sum
of tensor products, hence is generated by multiplication and Fubini.
The boundary coefficients are formal by \cite{LongPolynomialKZ}.  If
$b_a/c_a=b'_a/c'_a$, then $b_ac'_a-b'_ac_a=0$ in
$\Rcal_\partial$; the complete boundary theorem makes the two cleared
relations formally equal.
\end{proof}

\section{Local endpoint comparison and numerical specialization}
\label{sec:endpoint-comparison}

The orbit theorem is over the boundary fraction field.  To specialize at a
fixed algebraic parameter we must prove that clearing denominators does not
create a new relation at that parameter.  The normal boundary model and a
flatness argument give the required descent.

\subsection{Regular local coefficients}

Fix a nonsingular $\xi\in k^\times$ and put
\[
  B=B_{\partial,\xi},
  \qquad K=K_\partial,
  \qquad \pi=z-\xi.
\]
By \cref{prop:boundary-specialization-prime}, $B$ is a discrete valuation
ring.

\begin{lemma}[Regularity of affine shifts]
\label{lem:regular-affine-shifts}
The constant column reductions in \cref{thm:complete-affine-orbit} can be
chosen so that every affine shift $A_\lambda$ lies in $B$, entrywise.  Every
coefficient $\eta_a$ in \eqref{eq:eta-character} is a unit of $B$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a local fundamental matrix $Y_\lambda$ holomorphic and invertible at
$\xi$ and use the actual entire $E$-function column $U_\lambda$.  A split
shift is
$A_\lambda=U_\lambda-Y_\lambda C_\lambda$; it is holomorphic at $\xi$ and
belongs to $K$.  Under the embedding of $K$ into the meromorphic germs at
$\xi$, the DVR valuation is the order at $\xi$, so holomorphy gives
$A_\lambda\in B$.  For a saturated block, both
$\det Y_\lambda(\xi)$ and the selected nonzero constant minor
$\det C_\lambda$ are nonzero.  Hence every determinant ratio defining
$\eta_a$ has valuation zero.  Thus $\eta_a\in B^\times$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Flatness of the orbit model}

Let $L=\Lambda_{\det}\subset\Z^s$ and let
$\rho:L\to B^\times$ be $a\mapsto\eta_a$.  Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-lattice-ring}
  D_\rho
  =B[\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_s]/I_\rho,
  \qquad
  I_\rho=(\delta^{a^+}-\rho(a)\delta^{a^-}:a\in L).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Flat partial-character quotient]
\label{lem:flat-lattice-ring}
The $B$-module $D_\rho$ is free, hence flat.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The lattice $L$ defines a congruence on $\N^s$: two exponent vectors are
equivalent when their difference lies in $L$.  The character $\rho$ identifies
any two monomials in one congruence class by multiplication with a unit of
$B$, and the character identity makes this identification independent of the
chosen lattice path.  Choose one monomial representative in each congruence
class.  Reduction along the binomials expresses every monomial as a unique
unit multiple of its representative, and no nonzero element of $B$ kills a
representative.  These representatives form a $B$-basis.  Finite generation
of the ideal follows from noetherianity, equivalently from the finite Markov
basis theorem for lattice ideals \cite{EisenbudSturmfels}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Flat determinant morphism]
\label{lem:flat-determinant}
For every $n\ge1$, the homomorphism
\[
  B[\delta]\longrightarrow B[x_{ij}:1\le i,j\le n],
  \qquad \delta\longmapsto\det(x_{ij}),
\]
is flat.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Flatness is local on source and target.  The source local rings are regular,
and the target local rings are regular and hence Cohen--Macaulay.  Every
geometric fibre is the hypersurface $\det X=c$ in affine $n^2$-space; it has
pure dimension $n^2-1$, including at $c=0$.  The dimension formula therefore
holds at every local point.  Miracle flatness
\cite[Theorem~23.1]{MatsumuraCRT} gives the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Flat local orbit quotient]
\label{prop:flat-local-orbit}
Let $I_B$ be the ideal over $B$ generated by the regular affine-linear
relations and the determinant lattice relations of
\cref{thm:complete-affine-orbit}.  Then
\[
  B[\mathbf X]/I_B
\]
is $B$-flat.  Moreover, if $I_K$ denotes the complete generic relation ideal,
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ideal-contraction}
  I_K\cap B[\mathbf X]=I_B.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
After constant row and column operations, the linear part of the orbit ideal
is a coordinate ideal.  Translation by the affine shifts
$A_\lambda\in B$ is a polynomial automorphism and does not affect flatness.
The remaining coordinate ring is obtained from $D_\rho$ by base change along
the product of the determinant morphisms and by adjoining the unsaturated
matrix and translation coordinates.  The determinant morphisms are flat by
\cref{lem:flat-determinant}; $D_\rho$ is $B$-flat by
\cref{lem:flat-lattice-ring}; polynomial extension preserves flatness.
Thus the complete quotient is $B$-flat.

A flat module over the DVR $B$ is torsion-free and injects into its generic
fibre.  Therefore a polynomial in $B[\mathbf X]$ which lies in $I_K$ already
vanishes in the $B$-quotient, proving \eqref{eq:ideal-contraction}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The endpoint-complete formal algebra}

Let $\mathscr C$ be endpoint-complete and fix $\xi$ as above.  Start with the
commutative $k$-algebra generated by the flag symbols of $\mathscr C$, their
finite differential closures, and all boundary symbols in
$\Rcal_\partial$.  Localize only at boundary elements whose value at $\xi$
is nonzero.

\begin{definition}[Local endpoint-complete formal algebra]
\label{def:endpoint-formal-algebra}
The algebra $\Pcal^{\mathrm{ec}}_{\mathscr C,\xi}$ is the preceding localized
symbol algebra modulo the following relations:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(K\arabic*)},leftmargin=3.2em]
\item linearity in chains and forms, and algebraic pullback and trace;
\item multiplication, Cartesian product/Fubini, and the zero-dimensional
      exponential product and phase-shift identities;
\item the Weyl identities \eqref{eq:Weyl-Laplace} in the completed phase
      module and all differential consequences of its algebraic first-order
      system;
\item phase-horizontal polynomial Stokes, balanced face relations, punctual
      endpoint relations, and the complete compact polynomial-line formal
      relations;
\item the algebraic Jacobian-correspondence and relative extension relations
      supplied by \textup{(E4)--(E5)};
\item the cleared determinant-line relations
      \eqref{eq:cleared-det-binomial} for a finite Markov basis of
      $\Lambda_{\det}$.
\end{enumerate}
Its functional period map takes a symbol to the germ at $\xi$ of the
corresponding entire function.  Its numerical period map evaluates that germ
at $\xi$.
\end{definition}

Every listed operation is an identity of algebraic exponential periods.
The localization is harmless: its denominators are boundary functions known
to be nonzero at the chosen parameter.

\begin{theorem}[Local parameter-polynomial formal comparison]
\label{thm:local-PPFC}
For an endpoint-complete Stokes--Lefschetz collection, the kernel of the
functional period map on
$\Pcal^{\mathrm{ec}}_{\mathscr C,\xi}$ is zero.
Equivalently, every polynomial-coefficient functional relation in a finite
$E$-function closure, with coefficients regular at $\xi$, is generated by
\textup{(K1)--(K6)} over $B_{\partial,\xi}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{cor:endpoint-completion-algebraic}, all nonprimitive forcing terms
belong to the boundary algebra.  Conditions \textup{(E2)--(E4)} and
\cref{thm:Stokes-generation,prop:product-derived-group} give the product of
special-linear derived groups.  Condition \textup{(E5)} identifies the
complete affine-linear reduction with the formal relations in
\textup{(K1), (K3)--(K5)}.  The generic relation ideal is therefore exactly
the ideal of \cref{thm:complete-affine-orbit}.  Its nonlinear generators are
formal by \cref{prop:formal-det-line} and \textup{(K6)}.

All coefficients occurring in a relation regular at $\xi$ lie in the DVR
$B=B_{\partial,\xi}$.  By \cref{lem:regular-affine-shifts} the displayed
orbit generators also lie over $B$, and by
\cref{prop:flat-local-orbit} the generic ideal contracts to their $B$-ideal.
Thus no additional local functional relation exists.  Finally, the boundary
normal form of \cref{prop:boundary-normal-form} identifies the symbolic
boundary algebra with its actual function algebra, so the descent is
faithful.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Numerical endpoint-complete polynomial comparison]
\label{thm:endpoint-value}
Let $\xi\in k^\times$ be nonsingular.  The numerical period map
\[
  \per_{\xi}:\Pcal^{\mathrm{ec}}_{\mathscr C,\xi}\longrightarrow\C
\]
is injective.  Hence every polynomial relation over $k$ among the flag values
at $\xi$ is generated by \textup{(K1)--(K6)}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First clear the finitely many localized boundary denominators; their values at
$\xi$ are nonzero by construction.  Choose a finite first-order $E$-function
system containing the flag functions, the boundary functions, and the closure
coordinates.  Beukers' refinement of
Siegel--Shidlovskii lifts a prescribed polynomial relation among the values
at $\xi$ to a polynomial-coefficient functional relation of the same degree
whose specialization is the prescribed polynomial; see
\cref{thm:Beukers}.  The lifted relation is regular at $\xi$.  By
\cref{thm:local-PPFC} it is a combination of the local formal generators.
Specializing this formal combination at $z=\xi$ gives the original numerical
relation.  Boundary coefficients specialize faithfully by
\cref{prop:boundary-specialization-prime}.  Therefore the numerical kernel is
zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Complete one-layer theorem]
\label{cor:complete-one-layer}
Let $(D,q,\Omega)$ be one endpoint-complete flag datum and let
$\xi\in k^\times$ be nonsingular.  Every polynomial relation over $k$ among
$F_{D,q,\Omega}(\xi)$, its algebraically required closure coordinates, and
the associated compact boundary values is generated by
\textup{(K1)--(K5)}.  After eliminating the closure coordinates, every
relation in the single value $F_{D,q,\Omega}(\xi)$ over the boundary value
algebra is formal.  No determinant-line generator is needed.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If the primitive projection is zero, \cref{prop:observable-cyclic-column}
reduces the function to the boundary algebra.  Otherwise its rational
weight-one rank is even and at least two.  The cyclic first-order closure
supplies one affine particular-solution column, and its nonzero primitive
projection means that this column survives the affine-linear reduction.  By
\cref{cor:one-column-free}, one column cannot saturate a rank-$n$ block, so
$\Lambda_{\det}$ contributes no relation.  Apply \cref{thm:endpoint-value} and restrict its injective period map to the
subalgebra generated by the original flag value and the boundary values.
\end{proof}

\section{Examples and falsification checks}\label{sec:examples}

\subsection{A triangular elliptic endpoint-complete example}

Consider
\[
  q(x,y)=y^2-x^3+3x,
  \qquad
  E_t:\ y^2=x^3-3x+t.
\]
The only affine critical points are $(1,0)$ and $(-1,0)$, with critical
values $2$ and $-2$; both singular fibres are nodal.  The projective pencil is
\[
  Y^2Z-X^3+3XZ^2-tZ^3=0.
\]
Its unique point at infinity is $p_\infty=[0:1:0]$, and the derivative with
respect to $Z$ at $p_\infty$ is $Y^2=1$.  Hence the total pencil is smooth
near infinity and there is no vanishing cycle at infinity.  The two finite
vanishing cycles form a basis of the rank-two affine $H_1$.

At $t=0$ the branch points of the $x$-projection are
$-\sqrt3,0,\sqrt3$.  The lifts of the adjacent intervals
$[-\sqrt3,0]$ and $[0,\sqrt3]$ are vanishing cycles with intersection
$\pm1$.  The two opposite Stokes sectors in the Fourier transform therefore
supply the two root arrows of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ by the formula of
\cite[Theorem~5.4]{DHMS}.  Since the exponential factors $e^{2z}$ and
$e^{-2z}$ are distinct, the exponential torus separates them.  Thus
\textup{(E2)--(E3)} hold.

The vertical face has a quadratic restricted phase and hence a rank-one
homogeneous boundary module.  The two slanted faces have the same restricted
cubic phase
\[
  -u^3+\frac52u^2+\frac14u-\frac38,
\]
whose critical values differ by $28\sqrt7/27$, not by $4$, the difference
of the primitive exponential factors $2$ and $-2$.  Over the fixed exponential parameter $z$, a projective identification can
twist all exponential factors by one common rank-one character but cannot
dilate $z$ or change their pairwise differences.  Every exponential difference in a tensor construction of the cubic boundary
block lies in the lattice $(28\sqrt7/27)\Z$, whereas
$4\notin(28\sqrt7/27)\Z$.  Thus even the adjoint tensor categories have no
common projective simple quotient with the elliptic block, and
\textup{(E4)} holds.

Take the rational triangle
\[
  D=\operatorname{conv}
  \left\{
  \left(-\frac12,-1\right),
  \left(-\frac12,1\right),
  \left(\frac12,0\right)
  \right\}.
\]
The slice $E_0(\R)\cap D$ is a relative arc through $(0,0)$.  Near that
point,
\[
  x=-\frac{y^2}{3}+O(y^6).
\]
The simplex arc is tangent to the real $y$-axis, while the adjacent
vanishing cycle is tangent to the imaginary $y$-axis.  Their incidence is
$\pm1$, so \cref{cor:connected-observable} gives observability.  For a
single layer \textup{(E5)} is automatic.  Consequently this is an
endpoint-complete one-layer example, and
\cref{cor:complete-one-layer} applies to every polynomial amplitude.

For the special amplitude $2y\,dx\wedge dy$, the reduction can be seen
directly.  Taking $a=x$ gives
\[
  da\wedge dq=2y\,dx\wedge dy,
  \qquad
  2y e^{zq}\,dx\wedge dy=d\bigl(xe^{zq}dq\bigr).
\]
This checks that the completed theorem agrees with ordinary polynomial
relative Stokes when the primitive class is zero.

\subsection{The affine-rank threshold}

The smallest special-linear orbit calculations test the nonlinear part of
the proof.  For $n=2$ and one split column, $\SL_2$ is transitive on
$\Atwo\setminus\{0\}$, whose closure is all of $\Atwo$; hence there is no
polynomial relation.  With two independent split columns, the orbit is
\[
  \{X\in\operatorname{Mat}_{2\times2}:\det X=\det C\}.
\]
Thus the first possible nonlinear relation occurs exactly when the number of
independent split columns reaches the primitive rank.  If the determinant
image contains $\Gm$, even this equation disappears after taking the orbit
closure.  These two cases verify the dichotomy in
\cref{lem:SL-matrix-orbits,thm:complete-affine-orbit}.

\subsection{Why connected fibres are necessary}

Let
\[
  q=x^2,
  \qquad
  \omega=x\,dy.
\]
Every regular fibre is the disjoint union of the two lines
$x=\pm\sqrt t$, and $\omega$ is exact on each component.  If
$\omega=dR+a\,dq$ with polynomials $R,a$, comparison of $dy$ coefficients
gives $R=xy+h(x)$; comparison of $dx$ coefficients would require
$y+h'(x)+2xa=0$, impossible polynomially.  Thus topological relative
exactness need not be denominator-free when the fibres are disconnected.

\subsection{Why isomorphic absolute blocks are not enough}

Two identical weight-one local systems can carry different relative slice
normal functions.  Hodge rigidity identifies the absolute systems, but a
linear combination of the relative classes can be monodromy invariant without
being a face class.  This is the normal-function obstruction recorded in
\cref{rem:normal-function} and is why relative correspondence compatibility
is retained in \textup{(E5)}.  The endpoint theorem removes specialization
relations; it does not manufacture a missing geometric identification of
extension classes.

\subsection{Why outer affine linearity alone remains insufficient}

Let $Y_1,Y_2$ be algebraically independent antiderivatives normalized by
$Y_1(b)=Y_2(b)=0$.  Their variable-endpoint relation ideal is zero, but their
endpoint values satisfy two new linear equations.  Thus
\cref{thm:relative-AG} alone cannot imply a value theorem.  The repair in
\cref{sec:completed-phase,sec:Stokes-generation,sec:affine-orbits,sec:endpoint-comparison} uses additional information absent from this
example: the punctual completion, the primitive Stokes group, the actual
boundary function field, and flat local descent at the chosen parameter.

\section{Scope and remaining boundaries}\label{sec:scope}

There are two unconditional levels in the paper.

On the primitive observable Lefschetz locus, without the stronger endpoint
hypotheses, the paper proves the constant-coefficient functional chain
\[
\begin{array}{c}
\text{functional relation}
\\[1mm]\Downarrow
\\[-1mm]
\text{vanishing phase densities}
\\[1mm]\Downarrow
\\[-1mm]
\text{rooted Picard--Lefschetz observability}
\\[1mm]\Downarrow
\\[-1mm]
\text{fibrewise de Rham exactness}
\\[1mm]\Downarrow
\\[-1mm]
\text{Bonnet polynomial relative exactness}
\\[1mm]\Downarrow
\\[-1mm]
\text{compact polynomial-line face relations.}
\end{array}
\]

On the endpoint-complete Stokes--Lefschetz locus, the completed phase module
and Stokes calculation strengthen this to the full value-level polynomial
chain
\[
\begin{array}{c}
\text{numerical polynomial relation at }\xi
\\[1mm]\Downarrow\quad\text{Beukers}
\\[-1mm]
\text{$k[z]$-functional relation}
\\[1mm]\Downarrow\quad\text{completed Fourier--Laplace system}
\\[-1mm]
\text{affine special-linear orbit ideal}
\\[1mm]\Downarrow\quad\text{flat local descent}
\\[-1mm]
\text{formal Stokes, face, correspondence, and determinant-line relations.}
\end{array}
\]
For one endpoint-complete layer the determinant line never occurs, so the
terminal formal relations are exactly the completed linear operations and
the compact polynomial-line theorem.

The endpoint-complete hypotheses are stronger than admissible observability
and are not asserted to hold for every polynomial phase.  The remaining
geometric boundaries are the following.

First, several places at infinity produce a weight-one puncture or $1$-motive
constituent.  It can in principle be added to the completed module, but its
Stokes root quiver and affine extension classes require a separate
observability theorem.

Second, non-tame or resonant Fourier transforms can have repeated exponential
factors and larger Stokes blocks.  The root-extraction proof must then be
replaced by a block-level Stokes generation theorem; strong connectivity of
the rank-one quiver is no longer the correct condition.

Third, unrestricted isomorphic blocks retain the normal-function problem.
Absolute Hodge rigidity supplies Jacobian correspondences, but it does not by
itself identify the relative extension classes.  Condition \textup{(E5)} is
therefore load-bearing.

Finally, genuinely complex-valued phases on real two-chains have
two-dimensional phase support.  The real-line phase distribution and its
one-variable Fourier--Laplace completion do not apply.  No theorem for that
category is claimed here.
